# The AI desk, 2026-10-28

User: "lets add an ai section to connect mcp claude code, codex, cursor,
whatveer. this will send all followed wallets and scanner results to ai and
let users use ai to trade live too. like there can be an mcp section where all
followed trades go to ai for analysis and ai has final say on the trade, and
exit."

## 1. What was already there

Krypto Bot's MCP server came across in the fork intact: one loopback HTTP
endpoint (`electron/system/mcpServer.ts`), a bearer token, an Origin check, the
off → read → paper → live ladder, a spending budget, setup snippets for a dozen
clients, and 22 tools (`shared/mcp.ts`, `electron/engine/mcpTools.ts`;
docs/mcp-connection-2026-09-21.md). Only its panel was dropped in the fork, so
nobody could switch it on. The AI could already buy and sell within its budget.
It could not see the follows, and the follows never asked it anything.

## 2. What was added

- **The AI page** (top bar, `src/pages/Ai.tsx`): switch on, access level,
  "Your AI app" with the command or config to paste, the token (hidden until
  asked), the port, the spending limits, a starter prompt that runs the desk
  loop, the follows the AI decides for, the decisions waiting on it, and a log
  of every decision with its verdict, reason, time and what the follow did.
- **The feed** (`wait_for_events`): a long poll over every followed wallet's
  trade (`leader_trade`), every copy row including skips and their reasons
  (`copy`), every finished Scout scan with the board's top ten (`scan`), and
  the desk's own `decision` / `decided` events. Cursor paging; up to 2,000
  kept; a reader that falls behind is told how many it missed.
- **The gate** (per follow, Follow › Advanced › Your AI decides): each buy
  and/or each sell waits for `decide_trade`.
  - Entry: `buy`, `buy` smaller (`sizeSol` ≤ the follow's size), or `skip`.
    Asked after every filter and the delay, before the price is read, so the
    copy's lag (paper too) includes the AI's thinking — the timing line gains
    an `AI` stage.
  - Exit: `sell`, `sell` less (`percent`), or `hold`. A hold on take-profit,
    max hold, trailing or graduation quiets that rule on that coin for ten
    minutes — that rule only; the others are still judged on every tick —
    and their sells are asked every time. The quiet ends when the position
    closes.
  - `sell_copy` closes part or all of one follow's copy through the follow's
    own exit path, so its record closes like any exit.
- **Port**: 8797 by default (settings revision 8 moves an unused 8787), because
  Krypto Bot listens on 8787 and the two apps on one machine collided — found
  on this machine during the hands-on check.

## 3. The rules

1. **The AI can only narrow.** Skip, shrink, hold, sell less. It cannot buy
   a coin no follow offered, buy more than the follow's size, arm or edit a
   follow, or change a setting. Its own `buy_token` stays under the budget.
2. **Silence has an answer.** No AI polling in the last two minutes, a
   connection that may not decide this mode (paper follows need paper or
   live; live follows need live), or no verdict by the deadline (3–45 s,
   default 15): an entry is skipped, or copied if the user chose "Copy
   anyway"; an exit goes ahead as the rule said. A withdrawn request is never
   copied. Answers are immediate when nobody is listening, so a gated follow
   costs nothing while no AI runs.
3. **Protective exits do not wait.** Stop-loss, trailing stop and creator-sell
   fire at once unless the follow turns on "Stop-losses wait for the AI too".
4. **Every row says what the AI did.** `CopyTrade.ai` holds the verdict or
   why there was none; the AI page's log holds the question, the answer, the
   time it took and the result.
5. **A question is about the follow as it was asked.** Pausing a follow (or
   its own loss stop pausing it) withdraws its open entry questions;
   switching its mode or removing it withdraws all of them. An approval that
   lands after the follow changed is still refused: the follow is re-read
   after every wait and at the last gate before money leaves.
6. **A protective exit never waits behind a question.** A stop-loss,
   trailing stop or creator-sell that fires while an exit on the same coin is
   waiting on the AI withdraws that question (it then goes by its own rule)
   and runs next. Creator-sell and graduation exits that fire meanwhile run
   when the exit in flight finishes, rather than being dropped.

## 4. Costs, said plainly

- Waiting is lag. A copy that waits 5 s for an AI enters 5 s after the leader
  plus the usual path; the record measures it.
- The AI app must keep running its loop; every event it reads costs its own
  tokens, at its provider's price.
- Nothing has measured an AI deciding copies profitably. The app's own research
  says copying loses on average; the starter prompt says skipping is fine.

## 4b. Audit fixes (2026-10-28, the release audit)

The release audit found the first version of the desk could buy for a follow
paused during the wait, let a held max hold silence the trailing stop, drop a
creator-sell that fired while the AI was thinking, turn "sell 30 %" into
nothing on a follow with a 50 % minimum leader sell, and stall the feed after
an app restart (a cursor from the old run sat above every new event). All
five are fixed and tested: rules 5 and 6 above, `aiSized` exits skip the
minimum, and `poll` reads a cursor past the newest event as "from the start
of this run" (`restarted: true`). A decision event is never filtered out by
`kinds`. The exit request now carries the position's mode, and `sell_copy`
checks the copy's mode, so a live copy is never decided by a paper connection
after its follow was switched back to paper.

## 6. Checked by hand (tutorial profile, paper)

The AI page was switched on at Paper, and a paper follow of the Scout board's
busiest wallet was given "Your AI decides each buy / each sell". A script then
acted as the AI app over real MCP HTTP:
- `initialize` answered with server `krypto-mirror` and 26 tools.
- A wrong token got 401.
- It looped on `wait_for_events`.

Within about four minutes, 9 of their trades reached the feed. The app asked
4 buys and 2 sells:
- Half-size buys were opened at 0.025.
- Skips were recorded as "AI skipped — …".
- Holding their first sell kept the copy open.
- Their second sell went through.

The AI page's log matched the follow's rows 6 of 6. One finding came from the
first run: the follow's own filters (liquidity, entry age) skip before the AI
is asked. That is intended, because the AI only sees what the follow would
have copied, but it means a strict follow may rarely ask.

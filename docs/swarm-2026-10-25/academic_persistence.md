# Academic and quantitative evidence on trader-skill persistence and copyable wallet features (Solana memecoins, DEXs, and adjacent copy-trading markets)

Scope note. Searched arXiv, SSRN (via secondary coverage where SSRN blocked fetches), ACM, PLoS, IDEAS/RePEc, Bitquery/CoinGecko/Dune write-ups, and vendor pages on 2026-10-25. Internal Krypto Copy results (9.3M pump.fun curve trades, two periods) are treated as given and labelled "internal, measured" only where a comparison is useful; nothing below re-derives them. Labels: **peer-reviewed**, **preprint**, **working paper**, **analytics vendor** (not peer-reviewed), **vendor**, **press**. Where a fetch was blocked (SSRN 403, ACM DL 403, one unibas page, one Nansen page 404), that is stated.

## Q1. Which published studies measure persistence of individual trader returns in crypto/DEX/memecoin markets, at what horizons, with what effect sizes?

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study measures week-to-week persistence of individual wallet returns on pump.fun or any Solana memecoin venue; the closest crypto evidence is a 2026 Hyperliquid preprint (Spearman 0.52 between adjacent 10-day windows for a markout-based "informativeness" rank, not PnL) and a 2026 Polymarket working paper (44% of "skilled" accounts keep the label on a held-out half of their events, versus about 10% for mutual funds). Outside crypto, individual-trader persistence is real but modest (Taiwan day traders: top-500 by prior-year return earn 37.9 bps/day net the next year; online poker: top-decile stays top-decile 13.6–20.7% of the time against a 10% base), and on eToro 30-day rolling performance shows no autocorrelation beyond a shuffled null.

### Cited Findings
- **Hyperliquid (DEX perps), preprint, Aug 2026.** 147,113 wallets, 14.3M aggressive orders, $84.3B taker notional; wallets scored by notional-weighted mean 10-second midpoint markout of aggressive orders over a frozen 10-day window (July 1–10, 2026), minimum 100 qualifying orders; Spearman rank correlation 0.52 between the scoring window and the next 10-day window (July 11–20); of 231 top-decile wallets, 91.3% trade again in the validation window and the top decile's validation markout is about double the ninth decile's and roughly ten times the lower deciles; replicated on an independent December 2025 sample — [Zhai, "Public Trader Identity: Adverse Selection and Return Predictability", arXiv 2608.04373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04373); details from the [HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04373v3).
- The same paper measures predictive R², not trading profit: adding top-decile wallet activity to an anonymous price/quote/flow benchmark raises out-of-sample 1-second return R² from 10.88% to 12.31% (a 13.2% relative gain, t = 9.2), 1.6× the largest of 200 activity-matched placebo cohorts; the author states that converting this into profit "would require a dynamic execution and market-making model" beyond the paper's scope, and does not discuss copy-trading — [arXiv 2608.04373v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04373v3).
- **Polymarket (on-chain prediction market), working paper, revised 25 Apr 2026.** 1.72M accounts, 210,322 markets, ~$13.76B volume, 2023–2025; a sign-randomization test (each account's trade history re-run 10,000 times with buy/sell direction randomised at the event level, accounts with ≥10 events) classifies 3.14% (54,477) as "skilled winners", 29.0% lucky winners, 61.4% unlucky losers, 6.4% unskilled losers, 0.1% market makers; skilled winners plus market makers (<3.5% of accounts) capture >30% of gains, lucky winners capture ~69% — Gómez-Cram, Guo, Kung, Jensen, "Prediction Market Accuracy: Crowd Wisdom or Informed Minority?" (title per [Yale Insights, 10 Jun 2026](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/wisdom-of-the-few-prediction-markets-are-driven-by-small-number-of-skilled-traders)); numbers via [The Block, 26 Apr 2026](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study) and [arXiv 2605.02287 (methodological review)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02287). SSRN abstract 6617059 returned 403 on fetch.
- Persistence in that paper: 44% of accounts classified skilled on one half of their events are classified skilled on the other half (the split is a random split of each trader's events, not a time split, per Yale Insights), against about 10% in a parallel test on active mutual funds; only 12% of top raw-PnL earners are in the skilled set; about 60% of "lucky winners" revert to losses on a separate sample of events — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study); [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/wisdom-of-the-few-prediction-markets-are-driven-by-small-number-of-skilled-traders).
- A methodological review of three 2026 Polymarket informed-trading papers states the sign-randomization test measures "persistent directional skill conditional on opportunity selection", excludes one-shot accounts by design, and is "mechanism-ambiguous" across market categories (sports 42% of markets, politics 36% of volume, crypto 37% of markets) — [Nechepurenko, arXiv 2605.02287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02287).
- **eToro (CFD/FX social trading), preprint, Jul 2025.** 164,634 traders with ≥2 trades, Apr–Oct 2013, ~87.5M trades: the autocorrelation of 30-day rolling performance is indistinguishable from a shuffled null ("past performance does not predict future performance"), while popularity is persistent and slow-moving; Pearson correlation between 30-day performance and popularity 0.11 ± 0.01; mirror traders average −10.98 ± 0.41 bps ROI per trade vs +1.15 ± 0.94 bps for non-mirror traders; mirrored (copied) trades average −61.24 bps vs −6.88 bps for self-initiated trades — [Joseph, Riedl, Pentland, Moro, "When Influence Misleads", arXiv 2507.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01817) (HTML v2 read for numbers).
- **Taiwan day traders, peer-reviewed (J. Financial Markets 2014).** 1992–2006; traders ranked by year-y returns and tracked in y+1: top-500 earn ~61.3 bps/day gross and 37.9 bps net; bottom-ranked earn −11.5 gross / −28.9 bps net; fewer than 1% of day traders reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees — [Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill"](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf); a later version ("Learning Fast or Slow", with Zhang) puts predictably profitable day traders under 3% of traders and under 10% of volume — [PUC-Rio seminar copy](https://www.econ.puc-rio.br/uploads/adm/seminarios/Learning_fast_or_slow_180606.pdf).
- **Online poker, peer-reviewed (PLoS ONE 2015), the cleanest decile-transition template.** 456.1M player-hand observations, Oct 2009–Sep 2010; 36,570 players with ≥1,000 hands in the first six months, 20,632 active in the second: top decile → top decile 13.6% (bb/100 ranking) or 20.7% (ranking by a t-statistic-like "performance robustness measure"), bottom → bottom 19.6% / 18.5%, against a 10% no-persistence base; player-level Spearman between halves 0.074–0.104 (raw) and 0.091–0.148 (t-stat); OLS slope of period-2 on period-1 win rate 0.167–0.229 (R² 2–8%), Deming (error-corrected) slopes 1.16–1.39; skill dominates chance at ~1,500 hands under their 75% criterion — [Potter van Loon, van den Assem, van Dolder](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0115479).
- **Hyperliquid leaderboard, self-published tool (Jul 2026, not reviewed).** Of ~39,000 leaderboard accounts, "of the wallets that even look followable, only around 1 in 5 actually hold up" after gates for copy-lag survival on candle data, fees, de-levering, BTC-beta stripping, single-regime penalty and a persistence gate; no parameters or cross-period figures are published — [Blacklight, Codeberg](https://codeberg.org/lordmueksch/blacklight).
- **Centralised crypto copy-trading (industry, unverified).** A "90-day study of 100,000+ outcomes" on Binance, Bybit and MEXC is cited as 97.04% of lead traders profitable on their own accounts versus 43.61% delivering positive returns to followers; the original study could not be located and the figures come from exchange blogs — [KuCoin blog](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/is-crypto-copytrading-profitable-in-2026); [MyFundedCapital](https://myfundedcapital.com/is-copy-trading-profitable/).
- **pump.fun population-level profitability (analytics vendors, no persistence).** Bitquery: across one launch week per month Nov 2025–Aug 2026 (~2.9M "ordinary" buyers, unsold coins valued at zero, ~2-week follow), 74% ended with less SOL (range 70–79% across the 10 weeks), median loss $13, the best 1% took 56% of all gains and the top 10% took 95%; the article reports no data on wallets that won across multiple launch weeks — [Bitquery, verified 15 Oct 2026](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money). CoinGecko's realised-PnL series shows profitable-wallet share bottoming at 30.1% (Jun 2025) and rising to 73.3% (Apr 2026); Bitquery could not reproduce the April figure (got 27% using CoinGecko's netting on its own data) — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback); [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money).
- A Dune dashboard (Mar 2026) of ~1.4M wallets trading pump.fun tokens found 49–50.6% lost money that month and ~96% finished red or under $500 profit — [Bitget News summarising the dashboard](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605318230). An early-2025 Dune count found 55,012 of 13.4M wallets had realised ≥$10,000 and 294 had realised >$1M; pump.fun's co-founder disputed it as excluding post-graduation gains — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/300403/pump-fun-traders-millionaires).
- Solana "agent" tokens (preprint, May 2026): across 925,323 holders of 11 Solana agent treasuries, median returns were negative on every platform and the top 1% of wallets captured 81.4% of gains; no persistence analysis — [Yu, Zhao, Sui, arXiv 2605.29174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29174).
- arXiv 2607.02795 (pump.fun sniper cohorts) remains withdrawn: "all numerical results below are withdrawn pending a corrected analysis" (v4, Oct 2026) — [arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795). Cite the method only.

### Inferences
- Across every venue with a real test, rank persistence of individual outcomes sits in the 0.07–0.52 range depending on how much noise the ranking metric removes (poker raw 0.07–0.10; eToro ~0; Hyperliquid markout 0.52; internal pump.fun follower-median 0.48 at k=5, measured). The Hyperliquid number is high because the metric (10-second markout, ≥100 orders) is a low-noise trait, not a PnL; it is closer to the internal "style" traits (ρ 0.83–0.98) than to outcome persistence.
- The Polymarket 44% retention is not a forward test: events are split at random, so regime change and alpha decay are not in the number. It is an upper bound on what a weekly walk-forward would show.
- Every study that ranks by raw PnL finds it a poor skill proxy (Polymarket: 12% overlap of top earners with the skilled set; eToro: no autocorrelation; Dorfleitner: picking highest cumulative return "leads to large losses"). This matches the internal finding that ranking by the leader's own return is worse than random.

### Gaps
- No published week-over-week persistence measurement of wallet-level realised returns on pump.fun, PumpSwap or Raydium was found; the internal measurement appears to be the only one of its kind.
- The Gómez-Cram et al. paper itself could not be fetched (SSRN 403); all figures are from The Block, Yale Insights and the arXiv 2605.02287 review.
- No academic persistence study of Hyperliquid or centralised-exchange copy-trading leaders exists; the 97%/43.6% leader-vs-follower figures are unsourced exchange-blog numbers.

## Q2. Which wallet-level features predict NEXT-period return out of sample, and which are merely descriptive?

### Takeaway
The only out-of-sample wallet-level predictor set in the memecoin literature is the WWW'26 copy-trading paper (prior-trade average return, t-statistic of mean return, trade count, time since launch at entry, purchase price/amount/quantity; removing the wallet-history agent costs 11.3% AUC), but the paper reports no base rate and models a zero-latency copier. Across adjacent markets, the robust out-of-sample findings are: noise-penalised rankings (t-statistics, Sharpe, shrinkage) beat raw returns; high activity and more coins traded predict worse outcomes; popularity/follower count predicts nothing; entry timing relative to launch has a small monotone effect (34% vs 28% of coins sold at a profit for first-minute vs day-later buyers) that is far too small to overcome the base loss.

### Cited Findings
- **Features used to predict a wallet's next first-position profitability (pump.fun).** Table 1 of the WWW'26 paper: average return over previous trades (all, 1st–5th, 6th–10th, 11th–15th), number of trades, return standard deviation, t-statistic of mean return, time since last/first trade, time since token launch at entry, purchase price, amount and quantity, bot dummies, plus candlestick and comment inputs; label = sign of realised profit on the wallet's first position in a coin; chronological 70/15/15 split by first-buy time; ~6,000 projects (Flipside on-chain data plus scraped comments); win rate, PnL and holding period are not explicit features — [Luo, Feng, Xu, Liu, arXiv 2601.08641v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v3).
- Ablations (gross return = net + 1): without the wallet-history agent AUC 0.6382 (−11.3%), smart-money gross 1.0753, copier gross **0.9327**; without the coin agent AUC 0.6489, copier 0.9415; without the timing agent AUC 0.6943, copier 1.0124. XGBoost had the highest overall AUC; SHAP values were computed (Fig. 9) but the ranking is only in the figure; the base rate of profitable first positions is not reported — [arXiv 2601.08641v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v3).
- **Entry timing relative to launch (Solana launchpads, analytics vendor).** Share of coins sold at a profit by time from launch to first buy: first minute 34%, 1–10 min 33%, 10–60 min 32%, 1–24 h 29%, after a day 28%; "even the fastest buyers lost on about two coins in three" — [Bitquery, Oct 2026](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money).
- **Diversification and size (same source).** Share of buyers losing by number of coins: 1 coin 71%, 2–5 coins 76%, 6–20 coins 79%, 21+ coins 78%; by SOL deployed: 0.1–0.5 SOL 80%, 0.5–5 SOL 72%, 5+ SOL 72% — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money).
- **Noise-penalised ranking beats raw ranking (poker).** Ranking by a t-statistic (rake-corrected win rate ÷ its standard error) raised top-decile retention from 13.6% to 20.7% and cut top→bottom transitions from 12.3% to 5.4%; the PRM regression R² roughly doubled (4.9–8.1% vs 2.2–3.3%) — [Potter van Loon et al. 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0115479).
- **Sharpe over cumulative return; activity negative; followers uninformative (social trading, peer-reviewed).** "Choosing the traders with the highest accumulated returns leads to large losses", ranking by Sharpe ratio performs better, only risk-aware strategies produce positive gross returns, none produces positive abnormal returns after transaction costs under a Carhart four-factor model; panel analysis: highly active trading is negatively related to returns and neither follower count nor invested capital relates positively to returns ("no wisdom-of-the-crowd effect") — [Dorfleitner et al., "To follow or not to follow", QREF 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v70y2018icp160-171.html).
- **Popularity is not a predictor.** eToro: popularity–performance Pearson 0.11; copiers choose "overwhelmingly" on popularity; traders who frequently revise whom they mirror outperform static followers (performance rises with revision rate, linear-log slope 2.06 ± 0.28) — [arXiv 2507.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01817). Bybit/TraderWagon: popularity correlates with whatever the platform's default rank is (−0.533 to −0.647 with the default rank) and ~0 with win rate (−0.001/0.011 on TraderWagon) — [Kawai et al., CHI 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf).
- **Markout as the ranking variable (Hyperliquid).** Scoring wallets on 10-second post-trade midpoint markout with ≥100 orders gives Spearman 0.52 across adjacent 10-day windows and a top decile whose next-window markout is ~2× the ninth decile — [arXiv 2608.04373v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04373v3).
- **Prediction-market skill vs raw PnL.** Only 12% of top earners pass the sign-randomization skill test; ~60% of "lucky winners" revert to losses out of sample — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study).
- **Day-trader persistence predictors.** Prior-year return rank predicts next-year return (top 500: 37.9 bps/day net), but the authors stress fewer than 1% are reliably profitable — [Barber et al. 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf).
- **Vendor label construction (not validated).** Nansen's "Smart Trader" label uses a realised-profit threshold with consistency criteria and the firm calls behaviour-inferred labels its "noisiest category"; its published copy-trading backtest page now returns 404 and no independent out-of-sample validation of any "smart money" label was found — [Nansen methodology](https://nansen.ai/post/what-is-smart-money-in-crypto-a-detailed-look-into-our-methodology); [Nansen backtest page (404 on 2026-10-25)](https://www.nansen.ai/research/trading-crypto-with-nansen-smart-money).
- **Leader-side behaviour that moves against followers.** Leaders are more prone to the disposition effect than unfollowed traders and becoming a first-time leader increases it (difference-in-differences) — [Pelster & Hofmann, JBF 2018](https://ris.uni-paderborn.de/record/3520). Traders' daily win-ratio rises right after followers add capital, consistent with closing winners early to please investors — [Glaser & Risius, JIT 2018](https://aisel.aisnet.org/jit/vol33/iss1/3); [LSE summary](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/businessreview/?p=12832). Showing others' success and offering a copy option both raise subjects' risk-taking in an experimental market — [Apesteguia, Oechssler, Weidenholzer, Management Science 2020](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3508).
- **Token-level (not wallet-level) early features.** MemeTrans (40k+ Solana launches, 122 features incl. bundled-account traces) and "Catching the Rug" (6.4M Solana tokens, XGBoost on the first 5 minutes) predict token risk, not trader returns — [arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13480); [arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20271). The Lillo-group pump.fun paper predicts graduation from launch "structural and behavioral" variables conditioned on SOL locked, again at token level — [Marino, Naviglio, Tarantelli, Lillo, arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14860).

### Inferences
- What has out-of-sample support for predicting next-period outcome (direction only, all small): (a) a noise-penalised version of past realised outcome (t-stat/shrunk/Sharpe) — poker, Dorfleitner, Polymarket, internal; (b) lower activity / fewer coins — Dorfleitner, Bitquery cross-section, internal (own return % falls as k grows); (c) markout-style informativeness on venues with a continuous book — Hyperliquid only, no memecoin analogue published. What is descriptive only or anti-predictive: raw cumulative PnL, win rate, follower/copier count, leaderboard rank, popularity.
- Entry-timing-to-launch is a weak monotone feature (6 percentage points of coin-level win rate between first-minute and day-later buyers in Bitquery's cross-section) and, per the internal labels, trades off against reachability (sniper trips 74% reachable vs 88–92%). No source shows it flips the sign of follower returns.
- Funding-source age, activity regularity and sell discipline appear in no published out-of-sample test for memecoin wallets; they are proposed internally (H5, H7) but unvalidated anywhere external.

### Gaps
- The WWW'26 paper's SHAP ranking (Fig. 9) and the base rate of profitable first positions are not in the text and could not be read from the HTML; the paper's dataset date range is also not stated (the text mentions the Jan 2025 $TRUMP launch and that "training data ends in October 2023", which may refer to the LLM rather than the dataset — ambiguous).
- No study was found that predicts next-week wallet returns realisable by a lagged copier on any chain; the Hyperliquid paper explicitly declines to model execution.
- No independent validation of Nansen/GMGN/Kolscan "smart money" labels against forward returns.

## Q3. Informed or insider wallets (dev-linked, pre-launch funded, sniper/bundle clusters): are their returns real, persistent, and reachable by a follower trading seconds later?

### Takeaway
Insider returns are real and large in aggregate but belong overwhelmingly to the deployer's own bundle, not to independent snipers (Bitquery: devs and bundled wallets ≥$579M vs snipers $31.5M after fees on 8.2M launches, Oct 2025–Jun 2026), they are extracted within seconds to minutes (Pine Analytics: 55% of deployer-funded snipes fully sold within 1 minute, 85% within 5 minutes; devs' first sale within 1 minute on 65% of coins), and in prediction markets the flagged insider accounts are one-shot by construction (open before a single event, dormant after). Nothing published shows these returns are reachable by a follower entering after the insider's buy.

### Cited Findings
- **Dev bundles vs snipers (analytics vendor, 8.2M pump.fun launches, 30 Oct 2025–7 Oct 2026; profit figures for launches to 30 Jun 2026).** Dev's own wallets (block-0 buys inside the launch transaction, fee paid by dev, or within the 4 following transactions of a Jito bundle) were in block 0 on 21% of coins taking a median 10% of supply; sniper bots (block 0–2 buyers across ≥10 different devs) were in block 0 on 28%; devs and bundles made at least $579M after fees ($529M with unsold at zero) and were ahead on 71% of bundled launches; the best 1% of bundled launches made 65% of total profit; 25,882 sniper wallets spent $440M for $31.5M realised ($15.2M with unsold at zero), 54% of sniper wallets ahead, top-10 snipers $6.7M, top-100 $13.1M; "Devs and their bundles made at least 18 times as much as the sniper bots"; devs' first sale within a minute on 65% of coins and within a day on 90%; sniper block-0 presence fell from 39% of November launches to 22% of September launches; the article does not report whether individual snipers stayed profitable across months — [Bitquery, verified 10 Oct 2026](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles).
- **Deployer-funded snipers (Pine Analytics via BlockBeats/Bitget, Jun 2025).** >15,000 launches since 15 Mar 2025 sniped in the launch block by wallets directly funded by the deployer; 4,600+ sniper wallets, 10,400+ deployers; >15,000 SOL net realised profit, typical per-wallet gains 1–100 SOL, 87% "success rate"; 11% exited within 15 s, 55% within 1 minute, 85% within 5 minutes, >90% used 1–2 sell orders; hub-and-spoke funding and burner wallets common; multi-hop funding chains were not traced — [Bitget News (translation of Pine Analytics)](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604803448).
- **Bot taxonomy and effect on token outcomes (WWW'26).** Bundle bots (non-creator buys in the launch block) appear in "roughly one quarter" of ~6,000 projects and are weakly associated with lower maximum log return and shorter dump duration; sniper bots (non-creator buys within the first 5 blocks) are "widespread" with minimal observable performance effect; the authors argue position bots mainly "redistribute profits" rather than change project performance — [arXiv 2601.08641v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v3).
- **Prediction-market insiders are one-shot.** 1,950 Polymarket accounts flagged by a lifecycle heuristic (opened shortly before a single event, dormant after resolution) move prices 7–12× more per dollar than skilled traders but are "too concentrated in isolated events to drive overall accuracy"; mean profit $15,012.92, median $2,758.29; the heuristic's precision against ground truth is unreported — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study); [arXiv 2605.02287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02287). Mitts & Ofir's composite screen (>210,000 wallet-market pairs, 2023–early 2026) uses cross-sectional bet size, within-trader bet size, profitability, pre-event timing and directional concentration, with ~$143M estimated anomalous profit and no reported precision — [arXiv 2605.02287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02287).
- **Named-event insider cases (press, on-chain analytics).** FOCAI (Jan 2025): at least 15 suspected insider wallets turned $14,600 into >$20M (Lookonchain) — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/focai-memecoin-insider-trading-solana-profit). $TRUMP: Chainalysis found 58 wallets made >$10M each ($1.1B total) while ~764,000 wallets lost money — [CNBC via NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/58-crypto-wallets-have-made-millions-on-trumps-meme-coin-764000-have-lost-money-data-shows/3819865/).
- **Sandwich bots on the same coins.** ~$13M made by sandwich bots across Bitquery's 10 launch weeks, "ahead in all 10 launch weeks" — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money).
- **Hyperliquid "toxic" wallets.** The author uses "toxic" as a descriptive label for high-markout wallets and explicitly makes no claim that a wallet is one trader or trades on private information — [arXiv 2608.04373v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04373v3).
- Industry sniper-profit estimates conflict by orders of magnitude (Bitquery $31.5M over 8 months for all snipers vs Pine's 15,000 SOL per month for deployer-funded snipers alone); methods and populations differ — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles); [Bitget/Pine](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604803448).

### Inferences
- The profitable "informed" population on pump.fun is the deployer plus its funded wallets; their edge is structural (they are the counterparty and the exit) and their holding period is shorter than any realistic copy lag. A 2-second follower of such a wallet is, by the vendor numbers, buying into a position that is 55% likely to be fully sold within 60 seconds. This matches the internal "Creator of any coin today" label being the worst follower outcome (−16.2 vs −9.5, measured).
- Independent snipers are a thin, declining and only marginally profitable group (54% of wallets ahead, median profit unreported, top-100 wallets hold 42% of sniper profit), so selecting on "sniper" is selecting on survivorship.
- The CCS'26 "Meme Coin Factories" paper (internal) and WWW'26 bundle findings agree that bundle presence predicts worse token outcomes; that supports a bundle/creator-cluster exclusion filter rather than a follow signal.

### Gaps
- No source measures whether a lagged follower of insider wallets captures any return; all insider numbers are the insiders' own PnL.
- Bitquery does not report per-wallet persistence of sniper profits across months; Pine Analytics does not report cross-launch persistence either.
- arXiv 2607.02795 (persistent sniper cohorts) is still withdrawn, so no peer-reviewed cohort-persistence figure exists.

## Q4. What exactly does the WWW'26 paper find about bots and leader PnL, and what related leaderboard-gaming studies exist?

### Takeaway
The WWW'26 paper (arXiv 2601.08641) does not measure or claim that leaders' historical PnL is inflated by bump or comment bots; it classifies bots (bundle ~25% of projects, sniper "widespread", bump and comment bots unquantified), finds comment bots associated with better token metrics, warns that manipulators "select a historically profitable wallet to attract victims" via the "optics of profitability", and reports its +3% copier return under an immediate (zero-lag) copier whose ablations go to 0.93–0.94 gross. The direct evidence on leaderboard gaming is Kawai et al. (CHI 2024): on TraderWagon/Bybit, popularity tracks the platform's default rank (Pearson −0.53 to −0.65) and not win rate (~0), leaders on the leaderboard page earn over half to three quarters of total profit from copier commissions, portfolios have a median life of 2.9 days, and leaders run up to six portfolios and close losers.

### Cited Findings
- **Paper identity.** "Resisting Manipulative Bots in Meme Coin Copy Trading: A Multi-Agent Approach with Chain-of-Thought Reasoning", Luo, Feng, Xu, Liu; v1 13 Jan 2026, v3 5 Feb 2026; journal reference: Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference 2026 (WWW'26); cs.AI cross-listed q-fin.TR — [arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08641).
- **What it measures about bots.** Position-manipulation bots: bundle bots (non-creator buys in the launch block) in roughly one quarter of projects, weakly associated with lower max log return (ln P_max/P_launch) and shorter log dump duration (seconds from peak until liquidity falls to 10% of peak); sniper bots (buys within K = 5 blocks) widespread with minimal effect; "gradual bundles" not measured. Attention-manipulation bots: bump bots (repeated equal buy/sell flips, score threshold ξ = 50) less frequent than comment bots, no percentage; comment bots (LLM-classified hype comments) "associated with improved performance metrics", no percentage — [arXiv 2601.08641v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v3).
- **What it says about leader PnL.** It does not quantify PnL inflation. It states adversaries "fabricate" volume and sentiment via wash trading and comment bots to create "an illusion of demand", that manipulators may "select a historically profitable wallet to attract victims", and that the "optics of profitability" attract copiers; GMGN is cited as ranking "key opinion leader" wallets by returns or number of trades — [arXiv 2601.08641v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v3).
- **Copier model and headline numbers.** Lemma 1: copier trades immediately after the smart-money wallet against the post-trade reserve of a constant-product curve; Theorem 2: imitation penalty ε = R(S) − R(C) > 0, buy-cost ratio Y/(Y−2d); pump.fun 1% fee assumed; slippage and latency beyond "immediate" unspecified; smart money ~14%, copier ~3% per coin; statistic-driven baselines (XGBoost, LASSO, NN) have negative copier returns; the multi-agent system is "the only approach with a positive average copier return"; XGBoost nonetheless has the highest AUC — [arXiv 2601.08641v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v3).
- **Kawai et al., CHI 2024 ("Stranger Danger?").** Two perp copy-trading platforms, TraderWagon (executes on Binance Futures) and Bybit, data Oct 2022 through 2023 (portfolio data every 12 h → 10 min; positions every 7 days); TraderWagon 600–1,200 portfolios, ~$38M total entrusted, ~USD 1,000–2,000 per copier; Pearson correlation between popularity and rank: TraderWagon 30-day ROI rank −0.533 (before UI update) / −0.569 (after), life-long PnL −0.204 / −0.064, 30-day win rate −0.001 / 0.011; Bybit 7-day aggregate copiers' PnL (the default) −0.647, life-long PnL −0.574, 30-day ROI −0.251, 30-day win rate −0.259 — [CMU PDF](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf) (DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642715).
- Quantile regression (TraderWagon, n = 27,879 / 25,474 portfolio-observations): a 100% excess ROI raises copier count by at most 3–5%, whereas appearing on the leaderboard page raises median popularity 12.9–22.3% and explains 62.4–76.7% of popularity at the 90th percentile; a portfolio gains 6.1–6.7% popularity within half a day of reaching the first page and loses 1.3–1.4% within half a day of dropping off — [Kawai et al.](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf).
- Gaming mechanics: leaders may publish up to six independently-scored portfolios and close losers at will ("they try multiple investment strategies almost simultaneously, and only keep successful ones"); portfolios are short-lived (median 2.9 days, mean 16.1); only 44.0% of the 7-day-ROI top-20 are in the 30-day-ROI top-20 and 30.5% of Bybit's 7-day-ROI top-8 are in its 30-day-ROI top-8; leaders holding a leaderboard-page portfolio "make over half to three quarters of their total profit from copiers' commissions" (10% of copier profits, risk-free since leaders share profits not losses); high profit-source ratios come from very low direct profits, i.e. ROI-maximising with little capital at stake — [Kawai et al.](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf).
- On Bybit, median ROI and win rate decline only "slightly" with leaderboard rank; aggregate copier PnL falls exponentially with rank by construction, but per-copier PnL falls much less (~USD 125 on average beyond the top 50), so "the number of copiers a given portfolio has is the dominant factor for its ranking" — [Kawai et al.](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf).
- **LLM-agent vault leaderboard (preprint, Sep 2026, internal already cites).** On 3,505 user-funded agent vaults on Base memecoins and a Hyperliquid fleet, "neither fleet shows a directional edge" and a leaderboard render boundary routes capital (RD 1.75× at the top-3 cut) — [arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663).
- **Volume/leaderboard gaming elsewhere (analytics firms, press).** CryptoSlam flagged ≥$577M of Blur-linked NFT sales as wash trades during the points airdrop (Dune put Blur's wash share near 11%) and estimated ~99% of LooksRare volume as wash trading tied to LOOKS farming — [Forkast on Blur](https://forkast.news/at-least-us577-million-blur-linked-nft-sales-wash-trades-cryptoslam-says/); [Dune Digest](https://dunedigest.substack.com/p/dune-digest-68); [Forkast on LooksRare](https://forkast.news/wash-trading-nft-looksrare-inflate-prices-analyist/). A consumer guide claims some top Binance VIP leaders were found wash-trading and pump-and-dumping to skew rankings; no primary source was located — [BitDegree](https://www.bitdegree.org/crypto/tutorials/binance-copy-trading).
- Social-trading platforms' own ranking emphasis: eToro's Popular Investor filters highlight one-year performance — [Cambridge Finance & Society article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/finance-and-society/article/from-users-to-cocreators-how-social-trading-platforms-facilitate-retail-investor-and-trader-engagement/95C0F520566CD0B987379662EC3BF6FA).

### Inferences
- The internal shorthand "leader PnL inflated by bump/comment bots (WWW'26)" overstates the paper. The defensible paraphrase is: the WWW'26 authors treat bots as a threat to copiers and build bot features into coin selection; they find comment bots correlate with better token metrics; and they warn that profitable-looking wallets are used as bait. The quantitative inflation of leader PnL by wash volume is better supported by CCS'26 "Meme Coin Factories" (internal) than by WWW'26.
- Kawai et al. is the strongest external evidence for the internal design choice to gate live arming on self-observed follower-realisable returns rather than imported rankings: on both platforms the default rank was a popularity engine with ~0 correlation to win rate, and the commission structure pays leaders for appearing profitable briefly.
- The multi-portfolio "publish six, keep the winner" pattern maps directly onto pump.fun twin/burner wallets (internal H4): a wallet's clean record can be the survivor of several discarded wallets.

### Gaps
- Kawai et al. do not report a numeric correlation between leaderboard rank and leaders' future ROI (only the qualitative "poorly correlated with future returns" and Figure 13's slight median decline); the follower-return outcome by rank is reported only as per-copier PnL (~USD 125 beyond rank 50).
- No study quantifies how much of pump.fun leaderboard PnL (GMGN, Kolscan) is attributable to bot volume or to self-trading; no published audit of those leaderboards was found.

## Q5. Adjacent copy-trading markets (eToro, ZuluTrade, wikifolio, Collective2, Bybit, tipsters): does leader performance persist, how are leaders ranked, and which selection rules worked out of sample?

### Takeaway
Across eToro, ZuluTrade, ayondo, wikifolio and crypto perp platforms, no study finds that past raw returns or follower counts select leaders who outperform net of costs; the selection rules that did better out of sample are risk-adjusted (Sharpe) and risk-aware rankings (Dorfleitner et al. 2018), penalising high activity, and frequently re-selecting leaders (eToro "explorers"). Leaders' own behaviour deteriorates once followed (stronger disposition effect, early profit-taking after inflows), and platform leaderboards are gameable. Sports-tipster studies find prediction accuracy above chance that does not convert into money.

### Cited Findings
- **Selection rules, peer-reviewed.** Dorfleitner et al. (QREF 2018): highest-accumulated-return selection → large losses; Sharpe-ratio ranking better; only risk-aware strategies positive gross; none positive after costs (Carhart); activity negatively related to returns; followers/capital unrelated — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v70y2018icp160-171.html).
- **Doering, Neumann, Paul (2015 primer, SSRN/EFMA).** 150 signal providers with ≥50 followers at 31 Dec 2011 (52 eToro, 76 ZuluTrade, 14 ayondo, 8 Currensee), weekly returns for 2012: median weekly return 0.07% with 1.79% SD; the worst 50% produced zero return, the top 50% "merely twice" the return of mutual funds with roughly twice the volatility; signal providers lever 2–9×, 40–50% of signals are shorts; the primer contains no persistence test — [SSRN 2291421](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2291421); [EFMA 2015 full paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0306_fullpaper.pdf).
- **wikifolio (peer-reviewed).** 1,084 certificates issued 2012–13: no average outperformance under factor models; best performers earned significant short-term excess returns — [Oehler, Horn, Wendt, RFE 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v46y2016icp202-210.html). A later Zurich study of ~8,600 wikifolios (2012–2018) finds large (>€1M) certificates beat MSCI World cumulatively — [fundresearch.de summary](https://www.fundresearch.de/studie/Warum-grosse-Wikifolios-oft-besser-performen-als-Fonds.php). Neither tests whether past winners keep winning.
- **eToro persistence and follower outcomes.** 30-day rolling performance autocorrelation equals a shuffled null; mirror traders −10.98 bps vs non-mirror +1.15 bps per trade; mirrored trades −61.24 bps vs −6.88 bps; "explorers" who revise mirrors outperform "keepers" — [arXiv 2507.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01817). Earlier eToro work (2011–13) models popularity growth as popularity × perceived quality — [Krafft et al., arXiv 1406.7729](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7729).
- **Leader behaviour under observation.** Leaders show a stronger disposition effect than unfollowed traders; becoming a first-time leader raises it (DiD) — [Pelster & Hofmann, JBF 2018](https://ris.uni-paderborn.de/record/3520). Daily win-ratio rises after followers add capital — [Glaser & Risius, JIT 2018](https://aisel.aisnet.org/jit/vol33/iss1/3). Findings conflict with Lukas et al. (2017) and Gemayel et al. (2018), who found smaller disposition effects for signal providers; Pelster et al. (2019) attribute the conflict to platform design and compensation — [Paderborn TAF WP 043](https://en.wiwi.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin-wiwi/cetar/TAF_Working_Paper_Series/TAF_WP_043_MinhLyPelster_2019.pdf).
- **Copy option raises risk.** Experimental market: information on others' success raises risk-taking; the effect grows when direct copying is possible — [Apesteguia, Oechssler, Weidenholzer, Management Science 2020](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3508).
- **Crypto perp copy-trading leaderboards.** See Q4 for Kawai et al. (CHI 2024); default rank drives popularity, win-rate correlation ~0, 50–75% of leader profit from commissions — [CMU PDF](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf).
- **Retail crypto trading behaviour (JFE 2024).** ~200,000 eToro accounts, 2015–2019: the same traders are contrarian in stocks and gold but momentum-like in crypto; the paper is about behaviour and makes no skill claim — [Kogan, Makarov, Niessner, Schoar, NBER w31317](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31317).
- **Collective2 / Myfxbook.** Only a 2009 CXO Advisory snapshot was found: of 193 active Collective2 stock systems, ~18% matched or beat the S&P 500 over 30 days; Collective2's disclaimer notes results are hypothetical and exclude commissions and subscription fees — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=4128). No persistence study exists for either platform.
- **Sports tipsters.** Australian Rules Football, six seasons, 1,141 matches: experts picked more correct outcomes than random but "no difference in monetary outcome was observed for experts compared to random selection", and predictions did not improve over time — [PMC10627876](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10627876/). A systematic review of 9 betting studies concludes knowledge or skill does not play a significant role in sports-betting outcomes — [UKE review (German)](https://fis.uke.de/portal/en/publications/sportwetten-expertise-oder-gluck-ein-systematischer-review-uber-tippstudien(f5e1e59c-3b17-4b89-b5c6-24bb9320ab9e).html).
- **Industry follower outcomes.** 97.04% of leaders profitable vs 43.61% of followers positive (unverified 100k-outcome "study" cited by exchanges) — [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/is-crypto-copytrading-profitable-in-2026). Fomo (Solana copy app): 6.16% of 292,000 wallets finished in profit over three months (DWF Ventures; internal already cites) — [Yellow](https://yellow.com/news/fomo-copy-trading-94-percent-wallet-losses).

### Inferences
- The adjacent-market literature converges on three selection rules with out-of-sample support: rank on a risk-adjusted or noise-penalised metric rather than cumulative return; penalise trading intensity; re-select often. All three are consistent with the internal shrunk-follower-median result and with "style persists, outcomes don't".
- Every platform studied compensates leaders for being copied, not for copier outcomes (eToro follower-count payments capped at $20k/month; ZuluTrade volume-based; Bybit/TraderWagon 10–15% of copier profits with no loss-sharing). pump.fun KOLs are compensated by the token's creator-fee share and by selling into followers, which is a stronger version of the same misalignment.

### Gaps
- No published study tests a shrinkage or lower-confidence-bound ranking of leaders on any copy-trading platform; Dorfleitner's "risk-aware" strategies are the nearest.
- The 2015 primer and the 2016 wikifolio paper predate most platform changes; no 2024–2026 academic eToro/ZuluTrade persistence study was found (the eToro preprint uses 2013 data).

## Q6. Alpha decay: how fast does a documented on-chain edge erode once copied or crowded? Any measured half-lives?

### Takeaway
No study measures a half-life for a copied on-chain memecoin edge. The closest measured decay curves are: on Hyperliquid, the identity signal's predictive gain falls from 13.2% at 1 s to 3.0% at 30 s and a 200–300 ms feed delay cuts it to 10.2–9.1%; on AMMs, price-shock information diffuses within a single block; in equities, factor-crowding decay is hyperbolic and live performance of back-tested smart-beta fell from 2.8% to 0.7% excess. On pump.fun the only decay-adjacent numbers are cross-sectional (sniper presence fell from 39% to 22% of launches in ten months) or internal (follower return worsens monotonically with shadow count; GMGN's own copy model assumes 1.5%/s drift).

### Cited Findings
- **Decay with delay and horizon (Hyperliquid, preprint).** Ridge gain from top-decile wallet identity: 13.2% at 1 s, 10.0% at 2 s, 6.7% at 5 s, 5.2% at 10 s, 3.0% at 30 s (still distinguishable from zero); delaying all features 200 ms / 300 ms lowers the 1-s gain to 10.2% / 9.1% while the anonymous benchmark "falls more sharply"; the toxic cohort's increment exceeds every placebo draw through ten seconds — [arXiv 2608.04373v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04373v3).
- **Information diffuses within one block on AMMs (preprint, Apr 2026).** Event-aware forecasting on on-chain AMM protocols finds price-shock information is absorbed within a single block because MEV bots and keepers close gaps almost instantly — [arXiv 2604.20374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20374).
- **Crowding decay shape (equity factors, preprint, Dec 2025).** Over Fama-French factors 1963–2024, hyperbolic decay fits momentum better than linear or exponential; a crowding signal flags seven of eight factors as crowded, but crowding-based timing fails to beat a factor-momentum benchmark — [arXiv 2512.11913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11913). A May 2026 preprint models half-life = ln 2 / (natural mean-reversion + AI-accelerated decay) without DeFi validation — [arXiv 2605.23905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23905).
- **Backtest-to-live gap (equities, practitioner research).** Smart-beta indexes averaged 2.8% excess return in backtests and 0.7% over five live years — [Institutional Investor on Research Affiliates](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1505pvxpn8lny/research-affiliates-beware-of-backtesting).
- **pump.fun sniper competition.** Sniper block-0 presence fell from 39% of November 2025 launches to 22% of September 2026 launches; bundled-wallet spending rose from $56.4M (June) to $1.2B (September), 61% in single ≥1,000 SOL purchases, with ≥90% removed within three days on 98% of such launches — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles).
- **Solana MEV scale (vendor/conference, not reviewed).** Jito counted 90,445,905 successful arbitrages in a year at $1.58 average profit; nearly half of Solana sandwiches trace to one program — [Helius MEV report](https://helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report). Ghostlogs estimated $370–500M extracted by sandwiching over 16 months on >8.5B trades — [Solana Compass, Accelerate 2025](https://solanacompass.com/learn/accelerate-25/scale-or-die-at-accelerate-2025-the-state-of-solana-mev).
- **Vendor latency thresholds (unverified marketing).** An RPC vendor claims copy trading "works as intended" under 50 ms, slippage becomes noticeable at 100–300 ms and "most strategies stop being profitable between 300 and 800 milliseconds" — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/solana-rpc/for-copy-trading).
- **Copier crowding, simulation only.** An agent-based model finds fundamental copycats erode their own profits through negative feedback while technical copycats amplify volatility — [EmergentMind summary of ABM study](https://www.emergentmind.com/videos/mimic-investors-and-profitability-an-abm-study-5085b8df). No empirical copy-trading paper measures follower-count price impact.
- **Leaderboard turnover as decay proxy.** Only 44% overlap between 7-day and 30-day ROI top-20 (TraderWagon); portfolio median life 2.9 days — [Kawai et al.](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf).
- Internal, measured (not re-derived): follower outcomes worsen monotonically with crowd convergence; White Whale Labs' 2-minute-lag cluster entries lost 41% per token (n = 93); GMGN's copy backtest subtracts 1.5%/s × (0.3 + 0.7·under-$100k share) from the leader's ROI — `docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md`, `docs/swarm-2026-10-23/external.md` §4–5.

### Inferences
- The only measured on-chain decay curves are on the order of seconds (Hyperliquid) to one block (AMMs), i.e. faster than any desktop copier's 150 ms–2 s path. Nothing published supports a multi-day half-life for a memecoin wallet's edge; the evidence points to edges that are either structural (insider/MEV, not copyable) or exhausted within the leader's own transaction window.
- The sniper-presence decline (39% → 22%) is consistent with competition and dev-side evasion squeezing an independent-sniper edge within months, but it is cross-sectional, not a per-wallet decay measurement.

### Gaps
- No measured half-life of a copied wallet's follower-realisable return on any chain; no study varies copier count exogenously.
- No peer-reviewed Solana-specific MEV or sandwich-profitability study was found (results are Ethereum/L2 or vendor).

## Q7. Which statistical methods do these papers use to avoid the overfit-top-decile problem?

### Takeaway
The methods with a track record are: cross-sectional shrinkage of individual alphas toward the population (Jones & Shanken 2005), false-discovery-rate control over many traders (Barras, Scaillet & Wermers 2010, with the 2019 caveat that it under-detects when signal-to-noise is low), bootstrap luck-vs-skill tests with Harvey & Liu's 2022 correction of both prior bootstraps, per-account sign-randomization nulls with held-out retests (Polymarket), t-statistic/precision-weighted ranking plus measurement-error-corrected regressions and minimum-sample filters (poker), activity-matched placebo cohorts with frozen scoring/validation/test windows (Hyperliquid), and strict chronological splits (WWW'26, Barber et al.). All of them target the same failure the internal data shows (decile 9 beating decile 10).

### Cited Findings
- **Shrinkage across the cross-section.** Jones & Shanken (JFE 2005) estimate the mean and dispersion of alphas across funds and combine them with each fund's own regression alpha in a precision-weighted average, shrinking extreme estimates toward the aggregate — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v78y2005i3p507-552.html); [NBER w9392](https://nber.org/papers/w9392). Extensions: Huij & Verbeek (2007) Bayesian alphas from the full cross-section; Zhang, Ding & Zhou (2014) family-level priors — [Netspar WP](https://www.netspar.nl/wp-content/uploads/Cross-sectional_learning_and_short-run_persistence.pdf).
- **False-discovery control.** Barras, Scaillet & Wermers (JF 2010) separate zero-alpha, skilled and unskilled funds under cross-fund dependence; 75% of 2,076 US equity funds (1975–2006) are zero-alpha net of expenses and skilled funds nearly vanish by 2006 — [published PDF](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~wermers/FDR_published.pdf). Andrikogiannopoulou & Papakonstantinou (JF 2019) show the method is conservative under low power: in simulations 65% of funds with ±2% alphas are misclassified as zero-alpha — [summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/359431).
- **Bootstrap luck vs skill.** Harvey & Liu (JF 2022) show Fama-French (2010) undersamples and fails to reject zero alpha even with economically large alphas, while Kosowski et al. (2006) over-reject even when all alphas are zero, and propose a corrected bootstrap — [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P151_Luck_versus_skill.pdf). UK replication finds no net outperformance under either bootstrap — [Blake et al.](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10023444/1/New%20Evidence%20on%20Mutual%20Fund%20Performance%20-%20A%20Comparison%20of%20Alternative%20Bootstrap%20Methods.pdf).
- **Per-account randomisation null plus held-out retest.** Sign-randomisation (10,000 re-runs per account, direction flipped at event level, ≥10 events) yields a p-value per account; skill labels retested on a held-out half of events (44% retention); raw PnL rejected as a skill proxy because it is "circular" — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/wisdom-of-the-few-prediction-markets-are-driven-by-small-number-of-skilled-traders); [arXiv 2605.02287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02287).
- **t-statistic ranking, minimum history, attenuation correction.** Poker: ranking by win-rate ÷ standard error (equivalent to a t-statistic) raised top-decile retention from 13.6% to 20.7%; ≥1,000-hand filter; hand-weighted decile averages; WLS for heteroskedasticity; Deming regression to correct attenuation (slopes 0.167–0.229 OLS vs 1.16–1.39 Deming); fixed-size hand samples (1,000/5,000/10,000) as a robustness check — [Potter van Loon et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0115479).
- **Frozen windows and matched placebos.** Hyperliquid: wallet scoring July 1–10, validation July 11–20, model fit July 11–20, out-of-sample July 21–27; ≥100 orders; 200 placebo cohorts matched on deciles of notional × order count; replication on an independent December 2025 sample; split-half reliability reported alongside the 0.52 — [arXiv 2608.04373v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04373v3).
- **Chronological splits.** WWW'26: 70/15/15 split ordered by each trader's first-buy time — [arXiv 2601.08641v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v3). Barber et al.: rank in year y, evaluate in y+1 — [Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf). Kawai et al.: quantile regression because popularity and rank are heavily skewed and OLS "could miss important effects" — [CMU PDF](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf).
- **Conservative PnL accounting.** Bitquery values unsold coins at zero, excludes wallets that transferred coins out, requires self-paid fees and <100 swaps/day, follows each buyer ~2 weeks, and excludes tips and app fees (so "real losses are slightly larger") — [Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money).
- **Observation-pipeline bias.** Two pump.fun collection pipelines from one program overlapped on 5 of 623 vs 1,742 mints; "collector configuration … shapes which tokens an on-chain study observes" (internal already cites) — [arXiv 2609.18975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18975).
- **Dependence on sample size.** Internal, measured: split-half reliability of the follower median rises from ρ 0.31 at 3 trips to 0.55 at 40 and never reaches 0.7 — `docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md` §4.

### Inferences
- The poker PRM result (t-stat ranking roughly halves top→bottom flips) is the closest published analogue of the internal shrunk-median result (−4.7 vs −6.3 for raw median), and it argues for ranking on (median − population prior) scaled by a standard error, with a hard minimum-trip gate, rather than on the raw median.
- The Andrikogiannopoulou & Papakonstantinou warning matters here: with per-trade noise this large and 5–20 trips per week, FDR-style tests will label almost every wallet "zero alpha"; a scout should therefore report shrunk point estimates with lower bounds, not binary "skilled" labels.
- Placebo cohorts matched on activity (Hyperliquid) and random-buy controls matched on curve position and time of day (internal protocol) are the same idea; the Hyperliquid paper shows that reporting the ratio to the best placebo draw (1.6×) is a readable way to express it.

### Gaps
- No paper applies hierarchical Bayes or FDR control to wallet selection on any DEX; the methods above come from mutual funds, poker, prediction markets and one perp DEX.
- Harvey & Liu's corrected bootstrap has not been applied to trader-level crypto data in any source found.

# Rotating a portfolio of copy-trading leaders (Solana memecoins, as of 2026-10-25)

Scope: the allocation layer above a wallet scout. How many leaders, how to size each, when to add or retire one, and how online-learning / bandit / manager-selection methods cope with the best wallet changing every week. Evidence grades used below: **A** peer-reviewed or primary dataset; **B** preprint, working paper or platform-published data; **C** vendor, blog, forum or second-hand summary; **I** internal, measured (Krypto Copy tapes and replays; treated as given).

Internal facts cited throughout (all **I**): copying memecoin wallets loses after costs on average; best-ranked decile −4 to −6.5% median per copy at ~2 s lag; decile 9 beats decile 10 in three periods; style traits persist strongly (ρ 0.83–0.98) and outcomes moderately (ρ ≈ 0.48); crowd convergence worsens outcomes monotonically; mirroring sells beats fixed holds; no shortlisted wallet survived a full-week chain replay on 2026-10-24. Sources: [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md), [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md), [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md).

One correction to the brief: arXiv 2609.05663's "leaderboard render boundary" ranks **market symbols** (a nine-slot movers panel), not agents or wallets. The 1.75× discontinuity is the rate at which agents enter a symbol at the rank-3/rank-4 cut, so it is evidence about display-position bias in selection, not about leader-performance persistence ([arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)).

## Q1. Diversification across leaders with heavy-tailed, correlated copy returns

### Takeaway
A small basket (5–10 leaders, equal- or equal-risk-weighted) captures most of the achievable variance reduction and is the only weighting scheme that survives estimation error; but diversification only lowers variance, it does not repair a negative median, and because leaders converge on the same coins the effective number of independent bets is the number of distinct mints, not the number of wallets. The one hard rule the evidence supports is one open copy per mint across all leaders, first leader wins.

### Cited Findings
- Of 14 optimizing portfolio models across seven datasets, none consistently beat the 1/N rule on Sharpe, certainty-equivalent or turnover; the estimation window needed for mean-variance to beat 1/N is about 3,000 months for 25 assets and about 6,000 months for 50 (**A**) — [DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal, RFS 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html)
- Across 6,000+ hedge funds (1990–2001, random equal-weighted portfolios), "a small number of funds is sufficient to reap most of the diversification benefits, whatever the considered strategy", and too much diversification produces "undesirable side effects in the higher moments" ("diworsification") (**A**) — [Lhabitant & Learned 2002/2004](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ris/jofitr/1342.html); summary at [hedgefundalpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/hedge-fund-diversification-how-much-is-enough/)
- Diversification reverses for extremely heavy-tailed risks (tail exponent ≤ 1, infinite mean) and is robust only for moderately heavy tails; for dependent power-law risks with tail exponent ≤ 1 diversification does not reduce VaR (**A**) — [Ibragimov & Walden, Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/1/2624460/2/ibragimov_walden.pdf); [Ibragimov 2009, CAS abstract](https://www.casact.org/abstract/portfolio-diversification-and-value-risk-under-thick-tailedness); [Ibragimov & Prokhorov 2016, SPbU](https://pureportal.spbu.ru/en/publications/heavy-tails-and-copulas(e90bd9d1-35de-4668-9bb6-ed5ff2a7728a)/export.html)
- Social-trading signal providers (150 providers with ≥ 50 followers on eToro, ZuluTrade, ayondo, Currensee; 2012; weekly net returns): median weekly mean 0.07%, SD 1.79%; the worst 50% produced a zero return; the top 50% earned about twice the mutual-fund return at about twice the SD; returns are negatively skewed with excess kurtosis ≈ 2, so "care should be taken when applying mean-variance analysis" (**B**) — [Doering, Neumann & Paul, EFMA 2015 full paper](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0306_fullpaper.pdf); [SSRN 2291421](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2291421)
- Two or more followed top wallets buying the same mint within 5 minutes makes the follower's 60-minute median worse, monotonically: K=1 −14.9%, K=2 −23.2%, K=3 −28.7%, K=4 −32.1% (Sept; July same shape); 8,016 four-wallet convergences in one day (**I**) — [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md)
- The one shortlisted wallet that looked positive on a 30-trip sample (+0.81 SOL) lost −9.80 SOL over its full week (2,027 priced copies, median −22.5%, mean −4.8%); removing its 10 best copies (up to +1,461%) takes it to −16.4 SOL, i.e. the mean is carried by a handful of outliers (**I**) — [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)
- Entering two minutes after large wallet clusters bought: average loss 41% per token across 93 tokens, median −95%; across 22.8M first purchases, a take-profit at 2× and 40% stop-loss nets about −19% per trade after costs (**C**, press summary of the White Whale Labs "Audit of the Memecoin Casino") — [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2297445); [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/white-whale-labs-solana-meme-coin-trading-driven-by-cluster-wallets)
- 3,505 user-funded LLM-agent vaults trading Base memecoins for 21 days: median true return 0.492×, only 16.2% finished profitable; portfolio convergence across vaults rose (mean pairwise Jaccard 0.304 → 0.473), and in one herd event 1,544 of 3,454 active vaults bought the same token within an hour, peaking at 441 buys/min (**B**) — [arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)
- Shrunk follower median (k = 10 toward the population median) selected a top decile that scored −4.7% [−5.3, −4.2] next half-day vs a −9.9% baseline, with a CI about 4× tighter than the raw median's; decile 9 beat decile 10 (−4.8 vs −6.3) for the third period running (**I**) — [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)

### Inferences
- How many leaders: with equal weights and average pairwise correlation ρ, portfolio variance is σ²[1/N + ρ(1 − 1/N)], so the share of *diversifiable* variance removed is 1 − 1/N regardless of ρ: 5 leaders remove 80%, 8 remove 87.5%, 10 remove 90%. Beyond ~8 the marginal gain is small and each extra leader adds credits, latency contention and convergence exposure. Lhabitant's "small number" and DeMiguel's 1/N result both point to 5–10, equal- or equal-risk-weighted, never mean-variance-optimized on a week of data.
- Correlation cap: the internal K-curve says the second follower-wallet into a coin is a negative signal, not a confirmation. Rule: **one open copy per mint across all follows; if another followed wallet already holds or has bought the mint in the last 5 minutes, skip (K ≥ 2 → no entry)**. Also cap total concurrent open mints (the existing "max open" guard) so that one hot narrative cannot fill the whole book.
- Tail regime: internal copy returns are bounded below at −100% and the right tail (+1,461%) is fat but the mean exists, so this is Ibragimov's "moderately heavy" case where diversification still lowers variance. It does not help the median: the basket median stays near the per-copy median (−4 to −6%). Diversifying a negative-EV process makes the loss more predictable, not smaller.
- Memecoin-specific crypto-fund data on leader-basket diversification does not exist publicly; the closest analogues are the Doering non-normality finding (negative skew, kurtosis 2 at weekly frequency) and the LLM-vault herd data.

### Gaps
- No published dataset of per-copy follower returns across multiple leaders on Solana from which the pairwise correlation ρ could be read; the internal tape could compute it (same-mint overlap rate and return correlation between followed wallets) and this is the single number the basket size depends on.
- Ibragimov's results are stated for tail exponents; the tail exponent of internal per-copy returns has not been estimated.

## Q2. Bandits and online learning for leader selection, with paper follows as cheap exploration

### Takeaway
Because paper follows reveal every shortlisted wallet's copy return at near-zero cost, the problem is not a classic partial-feedback bandit: ranking is a full-information "experts" problem (Hedge / Fixed Share), the weekly shortlist cut is fixed-budget best-arm identification (Sequential Halving), and only the live-capital step is a bandit, which should be a *conservative* one that allocates by lower confidence bound and keeps a guaranteed baseline. Non-stationary theory (sliding-window or discounted estimates) gives the window formula but admits it cannot set the window when the number of changes is unknown, so the lookback must be tuned on the internal tape.

### Cited Findings
- Discounted UCB and Sliding-Window UCB for abruptly changing rewards: with horizon T, Υ_T breakpoints and rewards in [0, B], D-UCB uses γ = 1 − (4B)⁻¹√(Υ_T/T) and SW-UCB uses τ = 2B√(T·log T / Υ_T), both achieving regret O(√(Υ_T·T·log T)), within a log factor of the lower bound; the paper gives **no rule for choosing τ when the number of changes is unknown** and says tuning "may be adaptively set, using data-driven approaches" (**A**) — [Garivier & Moulines, arXiv 0805.3415](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0805.3415)
- Discounted Thompson Sampling (Gaussian priors) reaches regret Õ(√(T·B_T)) with B_T breakpoints; change-detection Thompson Sampling (KS / Anderson-Darling tests) is the actively adaptive alternative, and a secondary source reports active adaptation outperforming passive discounting in numerical studies (**B**) — [Qi et al., arXiv 2305.10718](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10718); [Zaid & Ghatak, arXiv 2009.02791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.02791)
- Fixed-budget best-arm identification: Successive Rejects (Audibert et al. 2010) drops one arm per phase; Sequential Halving (Karnin et al. 2013) drops half the remaining arms per round; both have error probability of order exp(−R·T/H₂) (**A**) — [Shahrampour et al., arXiv 1609.02606](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.02606); [Wang et al., arXiv 2312.12137](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.12137)
- Conservative bandits: the learner must keep cumulative reward above a fixed fraction of a baseline at all times; the algorithm plays the optimistic (UCB) arm only when a lower bound on the resulting cumulative reward still clears the baseline, otherwise plays the baseline; near-optimal in the stochastic case (**A**) — [Wu, Shariff, Lattimore & Szepesvári, ICML 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.04282); follow-ups [arXiv 2002.03221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.03221)
- Pessimism / lower confidence bound selection is adaptively minimax-optimal for offline bandits, reaching a 1/N rate on near-expert data versus the usual 1/√N (**A**) — [Rashidinejad et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/60ce36723c17bbac504f2ef4c8a46995-Abstract.html)
- Tracking the best expert (Fixed Share): regret against the best sequence of experts with S switches is O(√(S·T·log(KT/S))), i.e. the cost of following a changing leader grows with the square root of the number of switches (**A**) — [Herbster & Warmuth 1998](https://datalearner.com:8443/academic/journal-papers/0885-6125/volumes-and-issues/32/paper-detail/10765); bound summarized in [arXiv 1909.02187](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.02187)
- Thompson sampling over a set of classic portfolio strategies as arms, reward tied to risk preference; strong results on the authors' own datasets, no independent replication found (**B**) — [Zhu et al., arXiv 1911.05309](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05309)
- Thompson sampling achieves logarithmic expected regret in the stochastic bandit (**A**) — [Agrawal & Goyal 2011](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/1111.1797)
- Display position causally routes selection: a 1.75× (95% CI 1.49–2.06) discontinuity in entry rate at the top-3 cut of a rendered movers panel; "chase-state" entries after a +0.75% 1-hour move had trailing 4-hour returns of +136.5 bps but forward 4-hour returns of −6.9 bps (**B**) — [arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)
- Split-half reliability of the follower median by trips per half: 3 → ρ 0.31, 5 → 0.35, 8 → 0.42, 10 → 0.40, 20 → 0.45, 40 → 0.55; it never reaches 0.7 and flattens after 8–10 trips; the board therefore ranks only at ≥ 10 copied trips on a shrunk median (k = 10, prior −15.5%) (**I**) — [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)
- Scout survivorship bias: the Scout only records trades its live feed carried; on one wallet's Scout day, 10 recorded trips reappeared in the chain replay but the replay found 23, and most of the 13 extra were −85 to −100% dead coins, so the board over-ranks wallets whose coins survived (**I**) — [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)

### Inferences
- Arm = wallet; reward per copy r = net return of a 0.1 SOL paper copy at the measured slot lag, **clipped to [−1, +3] for the estimator only** (unclipped for accounting) so one +1,461% print cannot promote a wallet. Keep a per-wallet windowed mean m̂, robust scale ŝ (MAD or IQR) and count n over the window.
- Three layers, each with its own method:
  1. **Shortlist → paper (fixed budget, full information).** Start the week with 20–30 shortlisted wallets all in paper. Run Sequential Halving on a 7-day budget: after days 2, 4 and 6 drop the bottom half by LCB of the clipped mean. This is the fixed-budget BAI setting because paper credits, not capital, are the budget.
  2. **Paper → live (conservative bandit).** Promote only when LCB₉₅ = m̂ − 1.96·ŝ/√n > hurdle, with hurdle = round-trip platform fee plus slippage allowance (≈ +3% net per copy) and n ≥ 20 paper copies in the window. The "baseline arm" in the conservative-bandit sense is *copying nobody* (return 0); live capital goes to a wallet only while its lower bound beats that baseline. This operationalizes the internal recommendation that the board may recommend nobody.
  3. **Live weights (LCB-weighted, see Q4).** w_i ∝ max(0, LCB_i), capped per wallet.
- Non-stationarity: with T ≈ 700 copies per wallet-week and Υ_T = 1 change per week, the SW-UCB formula gives τ ≈ 2B·√(700·6.55) ≈ 135 copies for B = 1 and ≈ 540 for B = 4 (reward range after clipping). Theory therefore brackets the window between "a couple of days" and "the whole week" and cannot pick inside that range; the internal split-half reliability (needs ≥ 10 trips, better at 20–40) sets the floor at about 20 copies. A practical default is a 48–72 h window with an exponential half-life of ~2 days, re-tuned on the tape (the data-driven approach Garivier & Moulines point to).
- Change detection beats pure discounting in the literature, and the internal style traits (hold time, size, trade rate, ρ 0.83–0.98) are exactly the kind of stable statistics a KS test can watch: a significant shift in the wallet's hold-time distribution is a detectable "breakpoint" long before its return series proves anything (see Q3).
- Winner's curse is measured: decile 10 under-performs decile 9 three times. A pessimistic (LCB) selector automatically penalizes the small-n, high-mean wallets that fill decile 10, and the shrunk median does the same thing in Bayesian form. Do not promote on raw mean, raw median or own PnL.
- Position bias (1.75× at a rank-3 cut) argues for not showing a ranked "best wallet" list as the promotion mechanism in the UI; promote on the rule, display the rule's outcome.

### Gaps
- No published application of non-stationary bandits or BAI to trader or wallet selection with out-of-sample results was found; the Zhu et al. portfolio-strategy paper is the nearest and is author-tested only.
- The right clipping threshold and window half-life are not derivable from theory; both need a walk-forward run on the 9.3M-trade tapes (shortlist on day d, paper on d+1…d+7, record promotion precision).

## Q3. Retirement rules: when a good leader stops being worth copying

### Takeaway
The manager-selection literature is consistent that firing on past returns is noise-chasing (fired managers do as well as or better than their replacements), while the behavioral copy-trading literature shows leaders *change behaviour* once they gain followers (more disposition effect, more trading, more risk). Combined with the internal finding that style persists (ρ ≈ 0.9) but outcomes barely do (ρ ≈ 0.48), retirement should trigger on observable structural change — style drift, crowding, inactivity, bot-rate — with return drawdown kept only as a capital breaker, and losing streaks requiring at least 8 consecutive losses before they mean anything at a 35% win rate.

### Cited Findings
- 3,400 plan sponsors, 1994–2003: managers are hired after large excess returns and do not outperform afterward; post-termination returns of fired managers are statistically indistinguishable from zero and sometimes positive; in matched hire/fire round trips, keeping the fired manager would have done as well as the new hire (**A**) — [Goyal & Wahal, JF 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i4p1805-1847.html); secondary [ifa.com](https://www.ifa.com/pdfs/do-investment-committees-pick-winners.pdf)
- Using the usual three-year evaluation window, managers with poor recent performance subsequently beat those with superior recent performance by 2.28 pp/yr; firing rules based on 1% or 3% underperformance led to the fired managers outperforming the retained ones over 2–3 year windows; "if past performance is used at all… it is the best-performing managers who should be replaced" (**A**) — [Cornell, Hsu & Nanigian, JPM 2017](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20180321-095702727); summaries [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=32197), [ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-dont-buy-winners)
- Hedge-fund persistence is strongest at quarterly horizon and weakens toward yearly, "largely driven by losers" (**A**) — [Agarwal & Naik, JFQA 2000](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v35y2000i03p327-342_00.html)
- Mutual funds ranked by quarterly abnormal return: the top decile earns 39 bps the next quarter and the effect disappears at longer horizons (**A**) — [Bollen & Busse, RFS 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v18y2005i2p569-597.html)
- Taiwan day traders 1992–2006: the top 500 by prior-year return earned 61.3 bps/day gross (37.9 net) the next year while the bottom group lost −11.5 (−28.9 net); fewer than 1% of day traders reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees (**A**) — [Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, JFM 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- eToro leaders are more susceptible to the disposition effect than non-followed traders, and becoming a first-time leader *increases* it (difference-in-differences), explained by responsibility to followers, "a fear of losing followers when admitting a poor investment decision", or image management (**A**) — [Pelster & Hofmann, JBF 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v94y2018icp75-88.html)
- Social traders who draw attention from peers trade more and take more risk afterwards (**A**) — [Pelster & Breitmayer, JEBO 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v161y2019icp158-179.html)
- Investors' trading increases with peers' performance, and as a result their own performance decreases and return volatility increases (**A**) — [Klocke, Müller-Okesson, Hasso & Pelster](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/the-impact-of-peer-returns-in-social-trading/)
- On a social-trading platform with convex fees, traders set risk by distance to the high-water mark (more risk mitigation as the HWM nears) and rankings significantly affect risk-taking (**A**) — [Scheckenbach, Wimmer & Dorfleitner, QREF 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v82y2021icp239-259.html)
- Providing information on others' success raises risk-taking in an experimental asset market, more so when subjects can copy directly (**A**) — [Apesteguia, Oechssler & Weidenholzer, Management Science 2020](https://bse.eu/node/9553)
- wikifolio portfolios that were profitable before becoming investable "mostly cannot maintain their performance afterwards"; persistence is generally low (**B**, conference summary) — [Kooij, U. Zurich via Basel SWE](https://app.psychologie.unibas.ch/swe/?p=3334); 1,084 wikifolio certificates (2012–13) did not outperform the market on average (**A**) — [Oehler, Horn & Wendt, IRFA 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v46y2016icp202-210.html)
- Platform-level retirement rules: eToro's Popular Investor program requires a maximum weekly drawdown of −25% and a daily risk score ≤ 7 across tiers (**B**, vendor) — [eToro Popular Investor](https://etoro.com/popular-investor); eToro's copy stop-loss defaults to 40% of the copy, settable 5–95% (**B**, vendor, verified in the internal skeptic review) — [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md](../../docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md)
- Signal providers' drawdowns are short: 50% of providers have drawdown periods under ~7 weeks and recover in ~3 weeks (mutual funds ~9 and 4.5 weeks); only the upper half of providers exceeds mutual-fund maximum and average drawdown (**B**) — [Doering, Neumann & Paul 2015](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0306_fullpaper.pdf)
- Anecdotal leader decay: a ZuluTrade #1 provider fell to rank 3,640 within three weeks (**C**) — [babypips forum](https://forums.babypips.com/t/if-you-are-in-zulu-trade-you-must-see-this/40912); Collective2 strategies "fall off" once they have paying subscribers, a popular VIX strategy closed to subscribers because fills degraded, and developers rename and restart (**C**) — [Elite Trader thread](https://elitetrader.com/et/threads/c2-strategies-comments.306417/)
- Style persistence vs outcome persistence (H1 → H2 Spearman, wallets with ≥ 5 trips each half): median first-buy size 0.94, share of trips under 60 s 0.84, median hold 0.83, own win rate 0.53, follower median return 0.48, own return % 0.37; "style is nearly a fixed trait, outcomes persist only moderately" (**I**) — [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)
- 85.0% / 85.1% of a training day's profitable wallets traded again the next day (two periods) (**I**) — [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md)
- Vendor claim that slippage "compounds when many people copy the same wallet" and that a one-block delay can mean entering 10–50% higher (**C**, unmeasured) — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/learn/copy-trading)

### Inferences
Concrete rule set, in order of evidence strength:
1. **Style drift (strongest signal, I-grade persistence ρ ≥ 0.83).** Keep a 7-day baseline of median hold, median first-buy size and share of trips < 60 s. Over the last 20 trips, if median hold or median size moves by more than 2× (|log ratio| > 0.7) or the under-60 s share moves by more than 25 points, pause the follow (back to paper). A trait with ρ ≈ 0.9 does not double by chance; a doubling means a different operator, a different strategy, or a wallet reacting to being copied (Pelster's leaders trade more and hold losers longer once followed).
2. **Crowding (I-grade, monotonic).** Measure the follower's own K at entry (other *followed* wallets already in the mint within 5 min): skip at K ≥ 2. For retirement, track the number of distinct buyers landing within 2 s after the leader's buys; if the 24-hour median doubles against the wallet's 7-day baseline, the wallet is being copied at scale and the lag cost (already −4 to −6% at 2 s) will rise. There is no external measurement of this; it is the obvious proxy to build.
3. **Inactivity.** 85% of profitable wallets reappear the next day, so 48 hours without a copyable trip is unusual; demote to watchlist at 48 h, drop from the live set at 7 days.
4. **Bot rate.** Keep the existing > 40 trades/min exclusion; it is a style trait and therefore persistent.
5. **Drawdown as a breaker, not a selector.** Keep the per-follow daily loss stop and wallet stop as capital protection (eToro's analogues are 40% per copy and −25% weekly). Set the follow's stop from its own paper distribution: pause if realized loss on the follow exceeds 2× the wallet's worst 7-day paper day. Do **not** re-rank on this; Goyal–Wahal and Cornell show return-based firing replaces noise with noise.
6. **Losing streaks.** With win rates of 33–37% (internal), the chance of k straight losses is 0.65^k: 5 → 11.6%, 6 → 7.5%, 8 → 3.2%, 10 → 1.4%. A 5-loss streak is a weekly event for every honest wallet; require ≥ 8 before acting, and prefer the LCB rule from Q2, which already encodes this.
7. **Weekly re-qualification.** Every wallet in the live set must re-clear the promotion rule (LCB > hurdle at n ≥ 20 in the last 7 days of paper-priced trips) or it is soft-retired (stop new buys, keep mirroring sells on open positions).
- The adjacent-field evidence is clear that *who* was good last quarter is a weak guide and *who changed behaviour* is a stronger one; the memecoin horizon compresses "quarter" to "day", but the ordering of signals is the same.

### Gaps
- No academic measurement of ZuluTrade or Collective2 leader decay after subscriber growth was found; only forum anecdotes.
- The Kooij wikifolio persistence result is known only from a conference summary (no sample size, period or effect size).
- Pelster & Hofmann's claim (via Wikipedia) that "losses are usually higher for copied trades" could not be verified against the paper; the IDEAS abstract covers only the leaders' disposition effect.
- No external study quantifies how follower returns degrade as copier count rises; the internal 2 s post-buy buyer count is unmeasured so far.

## Q4. Sizing: fractional Kelly under heavy tails and estimation error, equal risk, LCB weighting

### Takeaway
Kelly with a negative expected log return prescribes zero, so live size is only defensible when the paper lower bound of the clipped mean clears the fee hurdle; above that, the literature's answer is a small fraction of Kelly (quarter to half) because estimation error, negative skew and kurtosis all push the true optimum below the formula, and half-Kelly already buys a 0.89 probability of doubling before halving at 75% of full-Kelly growth. For a 10 SOL copy bankroll with plausible positive-LCB parameters that lands at roughly 0.2 SOL per copy, spread over 5–8 leaders with hard caps.

### Cited Findings
- Kelly fraction vs probability of doubling before halving vs relative growth rate (2% edge): 0.2 → 0.998 / 0.36; 0.3 → 0.98 / 0.51; 0.4 → 0.94 / 0.64; **0.5 → 0.89 / 0.75**; 0.7 → 0.78 / 0.91; 1.0 → 0.67 / 1.00; 1.5 → 0.56 / 0.75; 2.0 → 0.50 / 0.00; "given the extreme sensitivity of E log calculations to errors in mean estimates… to be on the safe side, the size of the wagers should be reduced"; 700 wagers at a 14% advantage can still turn $1,000 into $18 under full Kelly, and half-Kelly into $145 (**A**) — [MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba, "Good and bad properties of the Kelly criterion"](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)
- Fractional Kelly equals Kelly blended with cash; for lognormal assets the negative-power utility δ·w^δ maps to Kelly fraction α = 1/(1 − δ) (half-Kelly is δ = −1, quarter-Kelly δ = −3), approximately correct for other distributions (**A**) — same source
- Under uncertainty in the win probability, Kelly bets should be shrunk; shrunken Kelly beats raw Kelly out of sample in simulation and tennis data; a "back of envelope" shrinkage factor exists (Beta-posterior model) (**A**) — [Baker & McHale, Decision Analysis 2013](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/deca.2013.0271); discussion of its robustness across uncertainty models (**C**) — [LessWrong "Never go full Kelly"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/TNWnK9g2EeRnQA8Dg/never-go-full-kelly)
- "Tail risks, downside skew and kurtosis suppress Kelly allocation and geometric growth"; a fractional Kelly criterion is derived from fat tails and subjective uncertainty (**A/B**) — [Turlakov, J. Investment Strategies](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-investment-strategies/5330296/leverage-and-uncertainty); [arXiv 1612.07194](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07194)
- Under heavy tails "optimal might not be so optimal in the end" (**A**) — [Geboers et al., Entropy 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/e25020202); an alternative weights positions by the cube root of return over kurtosis, giving "more diversified portfolios with a less pronounced winner-takes-all profile than that dictated by Kelly" (**B**) — [arXiv 2011.13637](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.13637)
- Equal-risk-contribution portfolios have volatility between minimum-variance and 1/N, avoid min-variance's concentration and 1/N's lack of risk control (**A**, via summary) — [Maillard, Roncalli & Teiletche, JPM 2010, MSCI summary](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/36c8ed57-cfad-4d77-8d40-6cbcc107a778)
- Signal-provider returns are negatively skewed with excess kurtosis ≈ 2, so mean-variance (and hence Gaussian Kelly) overstates performance (**B**) — [Doering, Neumann & Paul 2015](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0306_fullpaper.pdf)
- Internal proposal on record: bankroll B per chain, default size 5% of B, max 3 open per config and 6 overall, open exposure 30% of B, daily loss 15% of B (**I**) — [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md](../../docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md)
- Internal: median copy of the best decile is −4 to −6.5%; a wallet's weekly mean (−4.8%) hides a median of −22.5% and depends on 10 copies out of 2,027 (**I**) — [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)

### Inferences
- **Zero is the Kelly answer today.** For small f, f* ≈ μ/σ² with μ the mean net return per copy; with μ < 0 (every measured cohort) f* ≤ 0. Any live size while the paper LCB is below the hurdle is a deliberate loss for information, and should be labelled that way in the UI (a "probe" size, e.g. 0.03 SOL).
- **When a wallet's LCB clears the hurdle**, e.g. μ̂ = +3%, robust σ̂ = 60% per copy: f* ≈ 0.03/0.36 ≈ 8.3% of bankroll per copy; quarter-Kelly → 2.1% → 0.21 SOL on a 10 SOL copy bankroll, inside the 0.03–1 SOL range. Use **quarter-Kelly, not half**, because the mean estimate comes from ≤ 1 week of paper on a distribution with kurtosis far above Doering's 2 and the Kelly table shows the growth loss from under-betting (0.25 → 0.44 of full growth) is cheap next to the ruin tail of over-betting.
- **Per-leader weights:** w_i ∝ max(0, LCB_i) / ŝ_i (lower-bound expected return per unit of robust scale), normalized over promoted leaders, then multiplied by the total live fraction; this is ERC with a pessimistic return tilt. Cap any one leader at 30% of the live book and the live book at 30% of B (internal proposal).
- **Sizing relative to the leader ("% of their trade" with a cap):** keep the proportional rule for mirroring sells, but cap the copy at the Kelly-derived SOL amount and at a small share of the coin's SOL reserves so the follower's own buy is not the price move it then sells into (no external number; internal measurement needed).
- Equal-risk with a Gaussian σ is the wrong scale for returns bounded at −100% with a +1,000% tail; use MAD/IQR of clipped returns as ŝ, as the fat-tail Kelly papers imply.

### Gaps
- Baker & McHale's exact shrinkage formula and Osorio's Student-t Kelly leverage were behind paywalls; only the direction (shrink) is sourced.
- No published study sizes positions across a basket of copied traders; the ERC-with-LCB weighting above is an inference, not a tested method.

## Q5. Cadence and turnover: daily, weekly or event-driven refresh; cost of switching

### Takeaway
Persistence evidence at every scale says "shorter horizons persist better, and even they decay fast": quarterly beats yearly for funds and day traders, and internally half-day ρ ≈ 0.48 collapses to no survivor over a week. The defensible design is a weekly shortlist, daily paper re-scoring and event-driven retirement, with switching costs controlled by soft-retiring (keep mirroring sells on open positions) rather than hard-dropping, because the internal result that mirrored sells beat fixed holds means an orphaned position loses its exit signal.

### Cited Findings
- Hedge-fund persistence decreases from quarterly to yearly horizons (**A**) — [Agarwal & Naik 2000](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v35y2000i03p327-342_00.html); quarterly mutual-fund persistence of 39 bps disappears at longer horizons (**A**) — [Bollen & Busse 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v18y2005i2p569-597.html); three-year manager rankings mean-revert (**A**) — [Cornell, Hsu & Nanigian 2017](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20180321-095702727)
- Within one 10.65-hour tape, follower median return persists H1 → H2 at ρ 0.48 (k = 5) and 0.49 (k = 20); activity halved between halves (**I**) — [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)
- Over a full week, none of 12 shortlisted wallets was profitable to copy; 15 of 263 eligible wallets were above zero on the board, "about what luck alone produces in a sample that size"; the best wallet's five days were each negative (**I**) — [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)
- Investors' dollar-weighted returns lag their funds' total returns by about 1.2 pp/yr (1.6 for ETFs) over a decade because of the timing of purchases and sales; widest in sector funds, ~0.2 in allocation funds (**B**) — [Morningstar Mind the Gap 2025](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap-2025); summary [Morningstar Australia](https://www.morningstar.com.au/personal-finance/young-invested-are-you-sabotaging-your-etf-returns)
- Fired-and-replaced managers: no gain from the switch; a secondary estimate puts the three-year post-hire result at about −0.47% after fees and transition costs (**A/C**) — [Goyal & Wahal 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i4p1805-1847.html); [ifa.com](https://www.ifa.com/pdfs/do-investment-committees-pick-winners.pdf)
- Fixed Share regret grows as √S in the number S of leader switches (**A**) — [Herbster & Warmuth 1998](https://datalearner.com:8443/academic/journal-papers/0885-6125/volumes-and-issues/32/paper-detail/10765)
- A ±15-minute schedule offset flipped the sign of an identical agent's outcome (−88 vs +21 dollars), an indication of how large timing noise is at short horizons (**B**) — [arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)
- Mirroring the leader's sells beats fixed holds; the median leader hold is seconds to tens of seconds (6 s median in the convergence study; 0.1 min median mirrored position) (**I**) — [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md)
- Chain-priced replay runs ~5 legs/s (a 30-trip replay in ~12 s, ~600 credits) versus hours through the swap API, so a daily full-week re-check of 20–30 wallets is feasible (**I**) — [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)

### Inferences
- **Momentum vs reversion by horizon (internal + literature):** intra-day (≤ 1 day) follower outcome shows positive but weak persistence (ρ ≈ 0.48); one-week-ahead shows none that survives costs; multi-year manager data shows reversion. Nothing supports a lookback longer than ~3 days for ranking or a refresh slower than daily for scoring. The weekly cadence belongs to the *shortlist* (universe refresh, chain replay, survivorship fix), the daily cadence to *scores*, and events to *retirement*.
- **Switching cost is mostly exit risk, not entry cost.** Entry into a new leader costs nothing until it trades; exit costs the sell signal on every open position. Soft retirement (no new buys; mirrored sells continue; max-hold timer as backstop) removes most of the turnover cost. Hard removal only on bot-rate or breaker triggers.
- **Turnover budget:** cap live-set changes to ~2 promotions and ~2 retirements per week unless an event rule fires; Fixed Share's √S penalty and Morningstar's timing gap both say frequent discretionary switching is paid for in returns.
- Because paper replay is cheap on-chain, run the *whole* candidate week every day, not a 30-trip sample: the sample is what produced the +0.81 SOL false positive.

### Gaps
- No external study of day-ahead or week-ahead persistence of on-chain wallet PnL was found (Nansen's smart-DEX-trader note is quarterly and its page is no longer reachable).
- The day-to-day ρ of the shrunk follower median is still unmeasured internally (flagged as open item M8 in the wallet-finder doc).

## Q6. "Find a new wallet every week" vs "rotate a basket": which frame, and the end-to-end pipeline

### Takeaway
Every source that looks at picking the single best past performer finds a winner's curse (decile 10 < decile 9 internally; hired winners do not outperform; top performers fail to persist on wikifolio; 16% of vaults profitable), so the evidence favours a continuously re-qualified basket with pessimistic promotion over a weekly hunt for one wallet. The pipeline that follows from Q1–Q5 is: weekly chain-replayed shortlist → all-in paper with Sequential Halving → promote on LCB above hurdle → live at quarter-Kelly with one-copy-per-mint → event-driven soft retirement → weekly audit that may recommend nobody.

### Cited Findings
- Decile 9 beats decile 10 in three periods; "the extreme top is fitted to its own sample" (**I**) — [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)
- 15 of 263 eligible wallets above zero ≈ luck; the top-ranked wallet (2zaAtsk4) was −14.1% median per copy over 60 copies (**I**) — [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)
- Hired managers chosen on strong excess returns do not outperform post-hire (**A**) — [Goyal & Wahal 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i4p1805-1847.html); recent losers beat recent winners by 2.28 pp/yr (**A**) — [Cornell, Hsu & Nanigian 2017](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20180321-095702727)
- Fewer than 1% of day traders predictably profit net of fees, but the top-ranked group *does* persist for a year (**A**) — [Barber et al. 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- The worst 50% of social-trading signal providers earned zero in 2012; near-zero entry barriers make "a high number of uninformed charlatans among signal providers" expected (**B**) — [Doering, Neumann & Paul, SSRN 2291421](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2291421)
- wikifolio portfolios profitable before becoming investable mostly fail to maintain it (**B**) — [Kooij via Basel SWE](https://app.psychologie.unibas.ch/swe/?p=3334)
- Only 16.2% of 3,505 memecoin agent vaults finished profitable; 92% of deposited ETH was withdrawn within the window (**B**) — [arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)
- Sequential Halving halves the candidate set each round under a fixed sampling budget (**A**) — [arXiv 1609.02606](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.02606)
- Conservative bandit: explore only while a lower bound keeps you above the baseline (**A**) — [Wu et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.04282)
- Internal product recommendations already on record: chain pricing for replay, fix the board's survivorship bias, "let the board recommend nobody, rather than its least bad wallet" (**I**) — [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)

### Inferences
End-to-end weekly pipeline (all thresholds are starting points to be tuned on the tape):
1. **Universe (continuous).** Every wallet the Scout sees with ≥ 5 closed trips; style labels from 5 trips (size, hold, under-60 s share, trades/min).
2. **Shortlist (weekly, Monday 00:00 UTC, and whenever the live set drops below 4).** Filters: ≥ 10 copied trips in the past 7 days; trades/min < 40; median hold in the band that measured best (< 1 min bucket was least bad internally, but this must be re-checked per period); not in the top decile of own return % (that selector was *worse* than baseline at −14.3%). Rank on shrunk follower median (k = 10, prior −15.5%) computed from a **chain replay of the full week**, not the Scout record (survivorship fix). Take deciles 8–9 plus the top of decile 10 → 20–30 wallets.
3. **Paper (days 1–7, all shortlisted).** Chain-priced paper copies at the measured slot lag, clipped-return estimator, 48–72 h window. Sequential Halving: drop the bottom half by LCB on days 2, 4, 6.
4. **Promote (any day, rule-based, no leaderboard click-through).** LCB₉₅ of the clipped mean > +3% (fees + slippage hurdle), n ≥ 20 paper copies, no style-drift flag, post-buy crowding below baseline, K-overlap with current live set low (shares < 20% of mints with any live leader over the window). Live set capped at 5–8.
5. **Live.** Quarter-Kelly × LCB/scale weights; one open copy per mint across leaders; per-wallet cap 30% of live book; live book ≤ 30% of B; existing daily and wallet loss stops.
6. **Retire (event-driven).** Style drift (2× hold or size shift over 20 trips), crowding doubling, 48 h inactivity, bot rate, breaker. Soft retirement first: stop buys, keep mirroring sells, max-hold backstop. 7-day cooldown before re-qualification.
7. **Audit (weekly).** Pooled live LCB vs pooled paper LCB; if the pooled live LCB < 0 for two consecutive weeks, the system recommends nobody and all follows revert to paper. Report precision of promotions (share of promoted wallets with positive next-week realized paper return) as the pipeline's own KPI.
- The frame "find a new wallet every week" is the maximum-of-noisy-estimates frame that produced the 5Voxqwa8 false positive; the basket frame with pessimistic promotion is what Goyal–Wahal, Cornell and the internal decile result all point to. Barber's result is the one piece of evidence that a *persistent* top group exists (day traders, year horizon), which is why the pipeline keeps a paper layer open for it rather than concluding that no wallet can ever qualify.

### Gaps
- No published end-to-end evaluation of a shortlist → paper → promote → retire pipeline for copy trading; the precision KPI in step 7 has never been measured anywhere found.
- Whether 5–8 live leaders is reachable at all under the +3% LCB hurdle is unknown; it may be zero most weeks, which is itself the honest output.

## Q7. Positive-expectation conditions: regimes, cohorts, times when copying was positive

### Takeaway
No source measured positive follower returns in any regime; what exists is indirect: the share of profitable pump.fun wallets swung from 30% (June 2025) to 73% (April 2026) by realized PnL, launches cluster 15:00–22:00 UTC, graduation rates fell three-fold between late 2025 and mid-2026, and the internal tapes differ by period (mirrored copy median −4.06% Sept vs −6.64% July). This says *when* matters at least as much as *whom*, but the only operational "when" signal with evidence behind it is the pipeline's own pooled paper LCB, gating live copying on and off.

### Cited Findings
- Share of pump.fun / PumpSwap wallets with positive realized monthly PnL: 47.6% Apr 2024, 38.3% Dec 2024, 35.9% Feb 2025, **30.08% Jun 2025**, 40.85% Oct 2025, 50.08% Jan 2026, 56.83% Feb 2026, 70.00% Mar 2026, **73.28% Apr 2026**; monthly active wallets fell from 5.2M (May 2025) to 1.8M (Dec 2025); realized PnL only (unsold bags excluded, which "understates losses"), wallet-level netting, no bot filtering; the authors "cannot conclusively explain this reversal" and hypothesize unprofitable traders left (**B**) — [CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)
- Earlier Dune analysis: only ~0.412% of 13.55M pump.fun wallets realized more than $10,000 profit; 0.00217% (293 wallets) more than $1M; criticized for excluding post-graduation trades and unrealized gains, and ~30% of wallets made a single transaction (**C**, press on Dune data) — [Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/over-99-of-pump-fun-traders-miss-the-10k-mark); [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2161945)
- pump.fun launches show "a strong diurnal cycle" clustering 15:00–22:00 UTC (US morning / EU evening), tailing off through the Asia overnight; 36-hour snapshot 2026-04-23 averaged 2,108 new tokens/hour with a 20:00 UTC peak of 2,698 (**C**, vendor example) — [Bitquery Launch Pulse](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/mcp/trading/examples/pumpfun-launch-pulse/)
- 832,941 launches (May–June 2026): 0.198% graduated within 24 h, a 3.18× drop from 0.63% in Sept–Oct 2025; launches advertising Telegram graduated at 1.485% vs 0.166% without (**B**) — [Kamat, arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02823)
- Roughly 80% of graduates complete the curve within six hours and ~30% within 30 minutes (**C**) — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/pump-fun-first-3-minutes-graduation-math)
- Realized agent returns fall with volatility regime: median −10.6 bps in the calmest volatility sextile to −98.2 bps in the wildest, with leverage unchanged (perps fleet, not memecoins); venue short-horizon reversal ρ = −0.0252/day, negative on 36 of 47 days (**B**) — [arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)
- Period dependence in internal tapes: mirrored copies net of 0.85% had median −4.06% (Sept, 122,071 positions, 36.9% win) vs −6.64% (July, 198,813 positions, 33.3% win); random 60-minute buys −59.7% / −59.5%; following one top wallet −14.9% / −10.3%; the < 1-minute-hold leader bucket was least bad in both periods (−3.72% / −6.22%) while 10–60 min holds were worst (−10.94% / −15.16%) (**I**) — [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md)
- Between the halves of one tape, activity halved and every persistence figure "includes a change of session" (**I**) — [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)
- Baseline strategy on 22.8M first purchases (TP at 2×, SL at −40%) nets about −19% per trade after costs; cluster-wallet tokens doubled at 28–46% vs 17% for others, yet no "monetizable advantage" after delay (**C**, press on White Whale Labs) — [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2297445)
- Social-trading top-half providers achieved risk-adjusted returns similar to mutual funds in 2012 while the bottom half earned zero (**B**) — [Doering, Neumann & Paul 2015](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0306_fullpaper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The CoinGecko series is the only multi-month regime measure found; a 30% → 73% swing in profitable-wallet share, concentrated in Jan–Apr 2026, is large enough that a copy strategy tested only in a 30%-regime month would look structurally hopeless while the same wallets in a 70%-regime month might clear the hurdle. It is wallet-level realized PnL, not follower return, so it is a regime *indicator*, not a profit estimate.
- Candidate regime gates, in order of how directly they are measured: (1) pooled paper LCB over the last 48 h across the shortlist (direct, internal, cheap); (2) rolling 7-day share of profitable wallets in the Scout universe (CoinGecko-style, computable from the tape); (3) launches/hour and 24 h graduation rate (Bitquery / Kamat style); (4) convergence frequency (K ≥ 2 events per hour, internal).
- Time of day: launches peak 15:00–22:00 UTC, and the internal tapes already carry timestamps, so follower return by UTC hour is a one-script measurement that has not been run; no external source reports it. Until it is, do not gate on clock time.
- Cohort: the internal hold-bucket result (fast flippers least bad, 10–60 min holds worst) is the only cohort effect replicated across two periods; Kamat's Telegram-advertised 9× graduation lift is a coin-cohort signal that a follower could use as a filter on *which* leader buys to copy.
- "WHEN" is implementable today as the conservative-bandit baseline: live copying is on only while the pooled lower bound beats zero; that is the regime detector the evidence supports.

### Gaps
- No measured evidence anywhere that follower (copy) returns were positive in any regime, hour, weekday or coin cohort; the CoinGecko and Kamat series measure wallets and tokens, not followers.
- No hour-of-day or weekday analysis of memecoin returns was found (Bitquery only suggests the 7×24 query); the internal tape can answer it.
- Nansen smart-money cohort returns by period could not be retrieved (article URL redirects to a blog index).

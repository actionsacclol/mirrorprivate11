# Signal engineering and validation for weekly selection of copyable Solana memecoin wallets

Scope: a methods digest for a quant engineer. Treats "which wallets will be profitable to COPY next week" as short-horizon forecasting on heavy-tailed, non-stationary, survivorship-biased data. Internal measurements from the Krypto Copy repo are cited as "internal, measured" with file paths; everything else carries a URL. Suggestions are kept under "Inferences" and are not evidence. Date: 2026-10-25.

Internal baseline the whole digest must respect (internal, measured; `docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md` §4, `docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md` addendum, `docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md`, `shared/walletScore.ts`):
- 2 s lag, 1.5 %/side recorded cost model, fixed network fees repriced at copy size; fee floor −10.5 % at 0.05 SOL and −7.0 % at 0.1 SOL (`shared/walletScore.ts` `feeFloorPct`).
- No decile of copyable wallets positive after costs; best decile −4 to −6.5 % median per copy, population −10 % (half-day tape) and −15.5 to −19.5 % (per-wallet medians, five tapes), bottom −20 to −38 %.
- Shrunk follower median `(n·med + 10·prior)/(n+10)`, prior −15.5 %, rank floor 10 copied trips, is the best ranker of a dozen tried: H2 top decile −4.7 [−5.3, −4.2] vs raw median −6.3 [−8.7, −3.9]. Ranking by the leader's own return is worse than baseline (−14.3 vs −9.9).
- Decile 9 beat decile 10 in three periods. Style traits persist (ρ 0.83–0.98), outcomes moderately (ρ ≈ 0.48). Crowd convergence worsens results. Mirroring sells beats fixed holds.
- Scout survivorship: a wallet's one recorded day held 10 trips; the chain replay found 23, the extras mostly −85 to −100 % dead coins.

## Key question 1 — Which wallet features plausibly predict next-week follower return, and what is the evidence?

### Takeaway
The only external study that predicts memecoin wallet profitability from wallet history (arXiv 2601.08641) finds trailing per-coin returns, their dispersion and t-statistic, wallet age, purchase size and bot-exposure flags to be its most important features, yet its statistical models still produced negative copier returns under frictions; the internal tape agrees that style features are stable labels while outcome features are the noisy ranking key, and the external literature on fund and tipster selection says the robust part of any such ranking is avoiding the persistent bottom, not picking the top.

### Cited Findings

**Direct memecoin evidence**
- A 2026 Solana/pump.fun study on 6,000 meme coins (Flipside on-chain data) predicts "the sign of the wallet's realized profit in the corresponding meme coin" for each wallet's first position per coin. Feature set: historical returns averaged over all, 1st–5th, 6th–10th and 11th–15th prior coins; return standard deviation; return t-statistic; number of trades; time since first trade; time since last trade; time since launch; purchase price, amount and quantity; bot dummies (bundle, sniper, bump, comment). Normalised importances — XGBoost: Return 6th–10th (0.0948), Trader Purchase Amount (0.0863), Return Std. Dev. (0.0755); neural net: Time Since First Trade (0.2974), Return 6th–10th (0.1287), t-stat (0.1019); LASSO: Bump Bot (0.2333), Purchase Amount (0.1537), t-stat (0.1115). Bundle bots appear in about a quarter of projects and are "associated with lower returns and shorter dump durations"; sniper bots appear in most projects with limited performance impact. Abstract claims "an average return of 14% for identified smart-money trades" and "an estimated copier return of 3% per trade under realistic market frictions", but the text says the statistic-driven models (LASSO, XGBoost, MLP) yield negative copier returns and only the GPT-4o multi-agent system is positive; validation is a single chronological 70/15/15 split; lag and slippage assumptions are not stated in the visible text; survivorship is not discussed — [arXiv 2601.08641v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- White Whale Labs "The Audit Of The Memecoin Casino" (384 M swaps, 7.7 M wallets, >1 M pools since late August): base rate ~1/10 of coins with real trades doubled within 24 h; of 22.8 M first purchases 17 % doubled before a 40 % drop; doubling TP with 40 % SL lost ~19 % per transaction after costs. Cluster wallets (450–2,000) buy within 1–2 min of launch and double-before-40 %-drop 50–60 % vs 17 % for others. Entering 2 min behind large clusters: −41 % per coin across 93 coins, median −95 %; tracking only groups with positive records still −11 %; backtesting a single wallet −20 % per transaction; a few tight-knit groups ~+10 % on 16 coins. Neural network raised doubling rate from ~10 % to 26 % on 771 selections over 8 days yet every day was negative (mean −11 %, median −45 %, 55 % hit stop within 24 h). Conclusion: "no monetizable advantage"; meme coin trading "almost entirely driven by cluster wallets" — [ChainCatcher summary](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2297445); also [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/white-whale-labs-solana-meme-coin-trading-driven-by-cluster-wallets). Primary report not retrieved.
- Coin-level survival predictors exist and are strong: pump.fun launches advertising Telegram graduate at 1.485 % vs 0.166 % without (832,941 launches, May–June 2026, pooled graduation 0.198 %) — [Kamat, arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02823); graduation was ~0.63 % in Sept–Oct 2025 (threshold ~85 SOL raised, ~$69k cap) — [Marino et al., arXiv 2602.14860](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.14860).
- Internal label evidence (H1 vs H2, same tape, κ = stability, outcome = H2 follower median): creator of any coin today κ 1.00, −16.2 vs −9.5; long holder (median hold ≥ 5 min) κ 0.65, −14.6 vs −9.7; large size (median first buy ≥ 2 SOL) κ 0.80, −13.6 vs −9.8; bot κ 0.61, −11.1 vs −9.5; small size (< 0.1 SOL) κ 0.87, −11.3 vs −9.0; scalper (< 60 s) κ 0.66, −9.4 vs −11.7 (possibly a slippage artefact); sniper (≥ 50 % of create-known trips opened within 10 s) κ 0.82, −9.1 vs −10.2 but only 74 % reachable vs 88–92 %; bundle κ 0.80, −7.3 vs −10.3 (confounded with short holds); high-frequency κ 0.39 (session effect, not a trait) — internal, measured, [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)
- Internal persistence (Spearman ρ, k = 5 / k = 20 trips): median first-buy size 0.94/0.98; share of trips under 60 s 0.84/0.93; median hold 0.83/0.90; reachable % 0.63/0.79 ("persistent, useless as a ranker"); own win rate 0.53/0.73 (top decile copies no better than baseline); follower median return 0.48/0.49; copy score 0.47/0.54; own PnL 0.42/0.49; own return % 0.37/0.28 — internal, measured, same file.
- "Too fast" bites under 10 s, not 60 s: for 10–60 s holds the follower median barely moved from a 1 s to an 8 s lag (−14.5 → −13.4); for 0–10 s holds reach collapsed from 82 % to 11 % — internal, measured, same file.
- Crowding: shadow wallets buying 0.4–3 s after a leader on ≥ 30 % of its mints is the top-ranked negative-filter hypothesis (H3), with convergence shown to invert the signal monotonically — internal, [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md §5](../../docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md), [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md).

**Analogous markets (fund, social-trading, tipster selection)**
- Carhart's persistence: top decile of one-year returns earns +0.22 %/month one-factor alpha next year, bottom decile −0.45 %/month; attributed mostly to stock-level momentum; Choi and Zhao replicate 1963–1993 but find no significant persistence 1994–2018 — [Choi & Zhao, NBER w26707](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26707.pdf). Persistence among the worst funds is attributable to funds poor in both the current and prior year — [Berk & Tonks, NBER w13042](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13042.pdf).
- Harvey & Liu treat fund alphas as draws from a population and pool cross-sectional information into a density forecast per fund; past performance is "a weak guide" mainly because of estimation noise; their EB forecasts beat alternatives out of sample — [RFS 31(7) 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v31y2018i7p2499-2552..html), [NBER w22134](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22134).
- Social-trading leaders with followers show a stronger disposition effect than unfollowed traders; a difference-in-differences shows becoming a first-time advisor increases it — [Pelster & Hofmann, J. Banking & Finance 2018](https://ris.uni-paderborn.de/record/3520). eToro working paper: trades with many followers are less likely to be closed and more likely to be increased; "advice seekers seem to be attracted by advisors susceptible to the disposition effect" — [Pelster & Romero Gonzalez (VoxEU PDF)](https://voxeu.org/system/files/2023-02/Social%20media%20interactions%20and%20biases%20in%20investment%20decisions.pdf).
- Lab evidence: information on others' success raises risk-taking and the option to copy raises it further; "copy trading leads to excessive risk taking" — [Apesteguia, Oechssler & Weidenholzer, Management Science 2020](https://bse.eu/research/publications/copy-trading).
- MIT thesis on a copy-trading platform: leader style factors, average follower performance and recent maximum gains predict follower gains/losses at ~80–90 % accuracy; followers "foraging" across leaders protects against large losses but does not produce outsized returns — [Lu, MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/112852?show=full).
- eToro "Popularity and Performance" models popularity change as past popularity × perceived quality; it does not test whether past performance predicts future performance — [arXiv 1406.7729](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7729).
- Tipster literature: a horse-racing Monte Carlo found a bettor needs over 10,000 bets at net return above 9 % to be called an expert at the 5 % level after the house edge (URL from search snippet, paper not opened) — [J. Gambling Studies](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2Fs10899-013-9420-7.pdf); a 2021 systematic review of nine betting studies: "nearly all studies show that knowledge or other skills do not play a significant role" — [UKE review](https://fis.uke.de/portal/en/publications/sportwetten-expertise-oder-gluck-ein-systematischer-review-uber-tippstudien(f5e1e59c-3b17-4b89-b5c6-24bb9320ab9e).html); AFL study of 1,141 matches compared tipsters to random selection (results not visible) — [PMC10627876](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10627876/).
- Nansen's "smart money" is rule-based on realised PnL, holding patterns and behaviour, with "token diversity, timing, and risk management" listed as skill indicators; exact thresholds unpublished — [Nansen Academy](https://academy.nansen.ai/articles/2132837-smart-money-101), [Nansen blog](https://www.nansen.ai/post/what-defines-smart-money-in-the-cryptocurrency-market).
- Base rates: profitable-wallet share on pump.fun stayed below 50 % from April 2024 to late 2025 (30.1 % in June 2025) and rose to 73.3 % in April 2026 (CoinGecko) — [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/solana-based-meme-coin-launchpad-pump-fun-traders-see-turnaround-in-2026-coingecko/); of 13.55 M pump.fun wallets, 55,296 report > $10k realised profit; ~30 % have a single transaction — [Brave New Coin on Dune data](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/over-99-of-pump-fun-traders-miss-the-10k-mark).

### Inferences
Feature-by-feature verdict (E = external evidence, I = internal evidence, S = suggestion only):
- **Trailing follower-return windows by coin count (last 5, 6–10, 11–15 coins) plus their SD and t-stat** — E (2601.08641 top features), I (follower median ρ 0.48). Compute on follower-realised returns, not leader PnL. The "6th–10th" window ranking above the most recent five is consistent with the most recent trades being the noisiest (and the ones that got the wallet noticed).
- **Entry time since launch distribution** — E (clusters buy within 1–2 min; graduation 0.2–0.6 %), I (sniper trips 74 % reachable; 0–10 s holds unreachable). Use the wallet's median and 25th percentile of `entry − create` as both a reachability filter and a style label; do not rank on it.
- **Market cap / curve % at entry** — S with I support (controls matched on curvePct are the internal protocol). Candidate: median curvePct at entry and the share of entries after graduation (PumpSwap) where impact is lower.
- **Hold-time distribution and its stability** — I (ρ 0.83–0.90; long holders worse; sub-10 s unreachable). Use as a label and a reachability gate; a wallet whose hold distribution shifts week to week is a style break worth flagging (see Q5).
- **Partial-sell discipline / loss-cutting speed (disposition)** — E (leaders with followers hold losers; copiers are attracted to them), I untested (H7). Feature: PGR − PLR and loser/winner hold ratio over the wallet's own trips; expected sign negative for stronger disposition.
- **Position-sizing consistency** — I (size is the most persistent trait, ρ 0.94–0.98; both large ≥ 2 SOL and small < 0.1 SOL label worse), untested as a ranker (H2). Feature: CV of first-buy size, and the follower-return gap between the wallet's top-quintile-size buys and its others.
- **Distinct tokens per week (selectivity)** — S; Nansen lists token diversity as a skill indicator but gives no test. Internal `focus` check penalises ≥ 4 trips per mint (concentrated: thin sample, 25 wallets).
- **Share of trades too fast to copy (reachable %)** — I: persistent, useless as a ranker, essential as a gate (< 25 % flagged).
- **Rug / dead-coin rate** — I (the Scout bias was exactly these; FOLLOWER_FILL_STALE_MS settles a leg at the last mark after 10 min), E (bundle-bot coins dump faster). Feature: share of the wallet's coins with no print 10 min after its exit, computed from chain-complete data, not feed-covered data.
- **Creator reputation / coin metadata of coins bought** — E (Telegram-advertised launches graduate ~9× more often; bundle presence lowers returns). Aggregate coin-level survival predictors over the wallet's buys: mean predicted-graduation probability of the coins it picks is a plausible selectivity feature. Creator-of-coin wallets themselves: warn strongly (κ 1.00, −16.2 %).
- **Time-of-day regularity / routine vs opportunistic** — I untested (H5), no external evidence found. Feature: trades per active day and CV of inter-trade intervals.
- **Wallet age and funding** — E (time since first trade was the NN's top feature; funder-based cohort detection is H4). Feature: days since first trade, number of funders within 1–2 hops shared with other candidates (cluster membership).
- **Recent drawdown** — E (loser persistence is the robust part of fund persistence; bottom decile persistent internally at −38 %). Feature: worst rolling 20-copy median over the trailing window; expected sign negative.
- **Trend of the shrunk median across weeks** — S, supported by alpha-decay evidence (Q5): a declining shrunk median with rising shadow count is the signature of a crowded leader. Feature: slope of weekly shrunk median over the last 4 weeks, and Δ(shadow count).
- **Crowding (shadow wallets)** — I (top-ranked negative filter), E (2-min-late cluster copying −41 %). Expected sign strongly negative; also a candidate cause of alpha decay in any wallet the Scout itself publicises.

### Gaps
- No external study tests selectivity (distinct tokens per week), sizing consistency, time-of-day regularity, or partial-sell discipline as predictors of *follower* return on memecoins; only the internal H2/H5/H7 protocols exist, untested.
- 2601.08641's copier model (lag, slippage, size) is in an appendix not retrieved, and the positive +3 % result comes from an LLM agent, not a reproducible statistical feature set; its single 70/15/15 split is not walk-forward.
- The White Whale primary report was not found; all figures are from secondary coverage.
- No Carhart original table or decile transition matrix was retrieved.

## Key question 2 — Target definition: how to compute follower-realisable return at scale, and which statistic to target

### Takeaway
The internal evidence says the target must be the follower's own slot-lagged, cost-inclusive return per copy (ranking on leader return is worse than random), that the median is far more stable than the mean (one wallet's +0.81 SOL on a 30-trip sample became −9.80 SOL over its full week; dropping its 10 best copies moved the total by 6.6 SOL), and that dead coins must be settled at the last mark rather than dropped; external work says Sharpe-type targets need deflation for selection and non-normality, so a per-trade Sharpe is the wrong primary target for 10–50 heavy-tailed copies.

### Cited Findings
- Internal fill model: entry and exit at the first print at or after leader time + 2,000 ms on both legs (`FOLLOWER_LAG_MS`), a leg with no print within 10 min settles at the last mark (`FOLLOWER_FILL_STALE_MS`; before 2026-10-23 such trips were dropped, "which flattered wallets that ride coins to zero"), 1.5 %/side recorded, repriced at read time to pump's ~1.25 % curve fee + Krypt fee per side plus fixed fees at copy size (~7 % of a 0.05 SOL copy); slippage not modelled; score built without fixed fees so a wallet scores the same at every size — internal, [shared/walletScore.ts](../../shared/walletScore.ts).
- Chain-priced leg: Helius `getTransactionsForAddress` on the mint with `filters.slot.lte = leader slot + 9` (2 s at 211.6 ms slots), price from the last pump `TradeEvent` virtual reserves or PumpSwap executed price at or before that slot; ~5 legs/s in plain Node vs hours via the swap API; on 26 trips both methods priced, median difference 0.0 points, 16/26 within 10 points, the rest up to ±60 because a pump coin "can move hard within one second" — internal, measured, [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md).
- Mean vs median: wallet 5Voxqwa8, 30-trip sample +0.81 SOL total; full week 2,160 trips (2,027 priced, 29 too fast): median −22.5 %, mean −4.8 %, 36 % win, total −9.80 SOL, every day negative; without its 10 best copies (up to +1,461 %) −16.4 SOL — internal, measured, same file.
- The follower-realisable objective halves the loss of the leader-PnL objective (Sept −3.42 % vs −6.52 %; July −4.11 % vs −10.99 %) and lifts win rate ~10 points; at the 0.85 % pump floor the gross follower return of the top decile is −2.6/−3.3 %, so the fee is not the binding term — internal, measured, [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md addendum](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md).
- Protocol already written internally: own curve impact at 0.05/0.1/0.25 SOL from virtual reserves; a trip with no print for 10 min priced at −100 % with last print as sensitivity; open trips marked at window end; prints by the leader, its cluster and wash prints excluded; report median, 1 %-trimmed mean, win rate, block bootstrap by mint — internal, [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md §5](../../docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md).
- Deflated Sharpe ratio adjusts a Sharpe for selection under multiple testing and non-normal returns (skew, kurtosis) — [Bailey & López de Prado, SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551).
- Learning-to-rank papers argue that sorting regression or classification outputs is suboptimal when the task is the ordering, reporting about threefold higher Sharpe for cross-sectional momentum (equities, preprint) — [Poh, Lim, Zohren & Roberts, arXiv 2012.07149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07149); a 2025 benchmark compares pointwise, pairwise and listwise losses for stock ranking — [arXiv 2510.14156](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14156v1).
- 2601.08641 uses a binary label (sign of realised profit on the wallet's first position per coin), winsorised features at 2.5/97.5 % — [arXiv 2601.08641v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2).

### Inferences
- **Compute at scale with the chain-slot pricer**, not the swap API: at ~5 legs/s a candidate shortlist of 300 wallets × 100 trips × 2 legs ≈ 60,000 legs ≈ 3.3 h per week of labels, within the stated 100k backtest allowance; the swap API cannot label a week. Record the raw price ratio per leg (exit/entry gross) so any cost model can be applied afterwards (the repo already does this via `grossOfRecorded`).
- **Cost model for the label**: per side 1 % venue + 0.5 % platform (1.25 % on the curve), fixed 0.0035 SOL per round trip expressed as % of size, plus own impact from virtual reserves at the reference size. Publish labels at a *reference size* (0.1 SOL) so the fee floor is a known constant (−7.0 %) and rankings do not move with the size picker.
- **Dead coins**: settle at the last mark (what the live copier would meet) and report the −100 % variant as a sensitivity; never drop. Open trips at the week boundary: mark at the boundary print (look-ahead otherwise).
- **Primary target: pooled per-copy median** at the reference size, as shipped. It is the statistic that persisted (ρ ≈ 0.48) and the one a Bayesian shrinkage model has a prior for. **Secondary targets** to log for every wallet-week, because they answer different questions: (a) 5 %-winsorised mean (cap each copy at the 95th percentile of the population's returns, ~ +300 % is the kind of cap to measure) which tracks PnL better than the median without letting one +1,461 % copy decide; (b) probability the week's median clears the fee floor, as a relevance grade for a ranker; (c) the lower 80 % bootstrap bound of the median (H6). Do not use a per-trade Sharpe as the target: with 10–50 copies, kurtosis dominates and the DSR deflation would swamp the estimate.
- **Classification vs regression label**: a 3-grade relevance label per wallet-week (below population median / between population median and fee floor / above fee floor) is the natural LTR label and is robust to the tail; keep the median as the regression label for calibration.

### Gaps
- No external source measures slippage/impact for 0.03–1 SOL copies on pump.fun curves; the internal impact estimate (0.3–0.7 points at 0.05–0.1 SOL) is a model, not a fill log.
- The per-trade return IQR (needed for every sample-size formula below) is not published in the internal docs; it must be measured on the chain-complete labels.

## Key question 3 — Validation: walk-forward protocol, weeks needed, survivorship and look-ahead

### Takeaway
Use calendar-week walk-forward with purging of trips that straddle the week boundary, an ex-ante universe (every wallet with ≥ 10 copied trips in the feature window, including those that go silent), chain-complete labels so the feed's coverage cannot flatter a wallet, and a paired weekly comparison of selections; the arithmetic says a 4-point gap between two selections' weekly medians is resolvable in roughly 2–8 paired weeks depending on the between-week SD, while separating one wallet's own median from the decile needs on the order of 80–100 copies, which is why single-wallet picks must be shrunk.

### Cited Findings
- Purged cross-validation removes training observations whose label-formation window overlaps the test fold (before or after it); embargo additionally drops a fraction of observations after each test fold (e.g. 5 % of 1,000 observations → 50) to absorb serial correlation; CPCV applies both across many train/test block combinations to produce a distribution of backtest paths — [Wikipedia: Purged cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation); primary reference López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (2018), ch. 7.
- Probability of Backtest Overfitting via combinatorially symmetric CV: PBO is the share of splits where the in-sample winner lands in the bottom half out of sample — [Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253).
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu: with hundreds of tested factors a new finding needs t > 3.0, not 2.0; their framework allows correlated tests and missing data — [NBER w20592](https://nber.org/papers/w20592).
- McLean & Pontiff (97 predictors): returns 26 % lower out of sample and 58 % lower post-publication; the out-of-sample decline is "an upper bound estimate of data mining effects" — [McLean & Pontiff PDF](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf).
- Internal split-half reliability of the follower median: ρ 0.31 at 3 trips, 0.35 at 5, 0.42 at 8, 0.40 at 10, 0.45 at 20, 0.55 at 40 — flattens after 8–10 trips, never reaches 0.7; reachable share 0.44/0.57/0.70 at 5/10/20 — internal, measured, [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md).
- Internal half-day selections carry wallet-clustered bootstrap CIs; the shrunk-median top decile's CI half-width was ~0.55 points vs ~2.4 for the raw median ("about 4× tighter") — internal, measured, same file.
- Internal survivorship: of 263 eligible Scout wallets 15 ranked above zero, "about what luck alone produces in a sample that size"; the one that looked profitable on 30 trips lost −9.8 SOL over its full week; the Scout records only trades its live feed carried, and the chain replay of one wallet-day found 23 trips against 10 recorded, the extras mostly dead coins — internal, measured, [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md).
- Internal protocol already specifies: pre-registered thresholds, train day N / test frozen on N+1 in both day-pairs plus one fresh pair, random-buy control matched on curvePct and time of day, FDR across hypotheses, and "a result counts as positive only if it holds in every pair at 2 s lag" — internal, [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md §5](../../docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md).
- Asymptotic SE of a sample median is 1/(2·f(m)·√n) where f(m) is the density at the median — [Wikipedia: Median, sampling distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median#Sampling_distribution).

### Inferences

**Walk-forward protocol (concrete)**
1. Weeks are UTC Monday 00:00 boundaries. For target week *t*, the feature window is weeks *t−L … t−1* (L = 1, 2, 4 as variants; the internal persistence evidence is all at ≤ 1 day, so L must be learned).
2. **Universe at the boundary**: every wallet with ≥ 10 copied (follower-filled) trips in the feature window, under the 40 trades/min bot rule. Freeze the list before any week-*t* data is read. Wallets that trade nothing in week *t* stay in the universe with "no copies" (a pick of them earns 0 and costs nothing); wallets that drop out are not removed from the denominator.
3. **Labels** from the chain-slot pricer, not the feed record, for every universe wallet: per-copy returns at the reference size for trips *opened* in week *t*. Trips open at the boundary are marked at the boundary print, so a feature-window trip cannot carry week-*t* information.
4. **Purge**: drop from the feature window any trip whose leader sell lands in week *t* (its label-formation window overlaps the test week). **Embargo**: additionally drop feature-window trips opened in the last 10 min before the boundary (the dead-coin settle window), so a settle-at-last-mark leg cannot peek into week *t*.
5. Fit the ranker on all weeks < *t* (expanding), produce a top-N for week *t*, record the pooled median, winsorised mean, win rate and hit rate of the top-N, plus the same for the controls (Q7). Roll forward. Report the distribution over weeks, never the pooled grand total.
6. Run CPCV over the ranker variants (the repo has tried ~12) and report PBO; treat a variant with PBO > 0.5 as noise. Deflate any Sharpe-like weekly summary of the top-N series by the number of variants tried (DSR).
7. Multiple-testing hurdle for features: Benjamini–Hochberg across the feature set each refit, or the stricter t > 3 for any feature promoted into the default ranker.

**Weeks needed to separate a −2 % from a −6 % decile** (derivation; replace the assumed SDs with the measured ones after 4–6 weeks). Treat each week's top-decile pooled median as one observation; a paired design evaluates both selections on the same weeks. With δ = 4 points, α = 0.05 two-sided, power 0.8, weeks needed = (1.96 + 0.84)² · σ_d² / 16:
| between-week SD of the weekly median difference σ_d | paired weeks | unpaired weeks per arm |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | 1.1 (use ≥ 3) | 2.2 |
| 2 | 2.0 (use ≥ 3) | 3.9 |
| 3 | 4.4 | 8.8 |
| 4 | 7.8 | 15.7 |
| 6 | 17.7 | 35.3 |
The population median moved −15.5 to −19.5 across five internal tapes (SD ≈ 1.6 points), and decile medians moved by 1–3 points between the two addendum periods, so σ_d ≈ 2–4 is the plausible range: expect 3–8 paired weeks for a 4-point gap, and roughly 4× that for a 2-point gap. Always pair (same weeks, same universe).

**Within-week noise and the minimum-sample rule** (derivation under a logistic-shape assumption near the median: SE ≈ 0.455 · IQR / √n). Internal evidence pins the IQR: if the 263 eligible wallets had true medians at the population −15.5 % and n ≈ 10 copies, 15 of them above zero implies a per-wallet SE near 10 points, i.e. a per-trade IQR around 70 points (or genuine between-wallet dispersion, which is the same thing for the formula). With IQR 60–70:
- a wallet's own median at n = 10 has SE ≈ 8.6–10 points (95 % CI ± 17–20 points): a single wallet cannot be told from the population at 10 trips, which is why ranking on the raw median at the floor is ranking on noise;
- to call a wallet's median "above −6 %" when its truth is −2 % at a one-sided 90 % bound needs n ≈ 77 (IQR 60) to ~105 (IQR 70) independent copies;
- the pooled top-decile median on a half-day tape had CI half-width 0.55, implying an effective n of several thousand copies; a top-10 selection with ~1,000–3,000 copies per week has SE ≈ 0.5–1 point, so the weekly selection median is well measured and the between-week σ_d above is almost entirely regime noise, not sampling noise.
Rules that follow: rank only at ≥ 10 copied trips (keep), shrink everything below ~80 copies hard, and show "reliable" only above ~80 copies rather than the current 20 (revise once the IQR is measured).

**Survivorship and look-ahead corrections**
- Label from chain-complete data for every universe wallet; treat the Scout's feed-covered record as a *candidate generator* only. If chain labels are too expensive for the whole universe, label a random 20 % of the non-shortlisted universe each week so the baseline is unbiased and the shortlist's coverage bias can be estimated (coverage-weighted: weight each recorded trip by 1/coverage of its wallet-day).
- Report the share of each wallet's chain trips the feed saw ("coverage %") and the dead-coin share among the unseen trips; a wallet whose unseen trips are mostly dead coins is being flattered.
- Do not condition the universe on "seen trading in week *t*"; conditioning on activity in the label week is the look-ahead that makes a leaderboard look alive.

### Gaps
- No internal measurement yet of the between-week SD of a selection's median (only half-day and two day-pairs six weeks apart); the table above is a formula, not a result.
- The per-trade IQR is inferred from "15 of 263 above zero", not measured.
- No external source gives a purge/embargo recipe for trade-level labels with a fixed post-exit settle window; the 10-min embargo is a suggestion.

## Key question 4 — Shrinkage and ranking: empirical Bayes, lower bounds, conformal, choosing k and the prior

### Takeaway
The internal k = 10 shrinkage is a normal-normal empirical-Bayes posterior mean in disguise with k = σ²_trade/τ²_wallet; the principled move is to estimate τ² and the prior from the population every week rather than fix −15.5 %, rank on a lower posterior bound (the winner's-curse literature and the internal "decile 9 beats decile 10" both say the top of a raw ranking is the most upward-biased), and if a top-N with a false-discovery guarantee is wanted, conformal selection with BH on conformal p-values is the distribution-free tool.

### Cited Findings
- Harvey & Liu random-effects alpha population, EB density forecasts per fund, out-of-sample improvement over fund-by-fund OLS; the 2016 draft estimates over 10 % of funds have positive alpha even though few are believed to outperform — [RFS 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v31y2018i7p2499-2552..html), [NBER w22134](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22134).
- James–Stein/EB shrinkage in online experiments: Thompson sampling driven by EB estimates rather than raw MLEs raised the chance of allocating to the best arm by 30–40 % in some experiments — [arXiv 1904.12918](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12918).
- Winner's curse: selected A/B winners show upwardly biased effects and invalid CIs; Meta's Bayesian Hybrid Shrinkage uses experiment-specific shrinkage factors to correct it — [arXiv 2603.12867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12867). Shrinking each arm toward the grand mean and picking the highest shrunk value works, but the between-arm variance estimate is noisy when K is small and shrinkage can bias downward when one arm is genuinely far better — [arXiv 2605.18887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18887). A Biostatistics comparison found bootstrap and EB-with-convolution corrections best reduce selection bias "though improvements in feature ranking are limited" — [bioRxiv 2023.09.22.558978](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.09.22.558978v3).
- Conformal selection: wrap any predictor, build a conformal p-value per candidate, apply Benjamini–Hochberg; FDR control holds under joint exchangeability of calibration and test data, in finite samples and distribution-free; extensions handle covariate shift (weighted) and data-dependent model choice — [Jin & Candès, JMLR 2023 (NSF PAR)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10538790); [Neyman–Pearson power, arXiv 2502.16513](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16513v1); [hierarchical data e-values, arXiv 2501.02514](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.02514).
- Internal: shrunk median with k = 10 was the least-bad of a dozen selections (−4.7 vs copy score −6.5, baseline −9.9); the 25th percentile of follower returns did as well (−4.8); the prior −9.9 % that preceded −15.5 % "pulled thin, lucky records UP the ranking"; the prior varies by tape (−15.5, −18.2, −19.5, −19.0, −18.0) — internal, measured, [shared/walletScore.ts](../../shared/walletScore.ts), [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md).
- Internal: decile 9 beat decile 10 in three periods — "the extreme top is fitted to its own sample" — internal, measured, [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md addendum](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md).

### Inferences
- **Make k a measured quantity.** For per-trade returns with within-wallet SD σ and between-wallet SD of true medians τ, the normal-normal posterior mean is (n·x̄ + (σ²/τ²)·μ)/(n + σ²/τ²), i.e. the repo's formula with k = σ²/τ². Method-of-moments each week: σ̂² = pooled within-wallet variance of (robust-scaled) per-copy returns; τ̂² = Var_i(x̄_i) − mean_i(σ̂²/n_i), floored at a small positive value. Illustration: σ = 30–40 points with τ = 8–12 points gives k ≈ 6–25, bracketing the internal 10; σ = 40 with τ = 5 gives k = 64 (shrink nearly everything). Because the prior μ moved 4 points across five tapes, estimate μ weekly from the universe, not a constant.
- **Heavy tails**: use a Student-t likelihood (ν ≈ 3–5) or shrink the *median* with the median's SE (0.455·IQR/√n) in place of σ/√n; the posterior for a location under a t likelihood is not closed-form but a two-step (robust scale, then normal-normal on the median) is adequate and keeps the k-formula.
- **Rank on a lower bound, not the posterior mean**: score = shrunk median − z · SE_post with z ≈ 0.84 (80 %) to 1.28 (90 %); this is the H6 "lower 80 % bootstrap bound" and the pessimistic rule that avoids the decile-10 overfit. For an offline selection problem (no exploration budget: the user bears every loss) LCB is the right side of the bound; UCB-style optimism is for exploration (Q5).
- **Hierarchical structure worth adding**: a two-level prior by style bucket (hold-time tercile × size tercile), since style is a near-fixed trait (ρ 0.83–0.98) and labels differ by 3–7 points across buckets; a wallet is shrunk toward its bucket's mean, the bucket toward the population. This is Harvey & Liu's random-effects idea with one extra level.
- **Conformal top-N with FDR control**: define the null "next-week median ≤ fee floor", calibrate conformal p-values on the previous weeks' (feature, realised median) pairs, apply BH at q = 0.2; the selection set may be empty, which is the honest "recommend nobody" the copy-candidates note asks for. Caveat: weekly regime drift breaks exchangeability; use the weighted (covariate-shift) variant with weights from a week-level density ratio, or recalibrate on the last 4–8 weeks only.
- **Choose k, z and q by walk-forward CPCV**, reporting PBO over the grid; do not pick on one tape (the dozen-variant internal pick is itself a multiple-testing exposure).

### Gaps
- No source found applying LCB/UCB selection with EB shrinkage specifically to trader or leader ranking; the LCB recommendation is an inference from the bandit and winner's-curse literature.
- Conformal selection under weekly non-exchangeability has no off-the-shelf guarantee; weighted conformal selection requires a density-ratio model that has not been built.
- Robust (Student-t) hierarchical fits for per-trade medians were not found applied to crypto wallets.

## Key question 5 — Non-stationarity: detecting regime shifts in which styles work, and the right rotation speed

### Takeaway
The environment itself is non-stationary at the base-rate level (pump.fun graduation fell 3.18× between late 2025 and mid 2026; the profitable-wallet share swung from 30 % to 73 %; the internal population median moved 4 points across five tapes), and alpha decays once a signal is acted on (58 % post-publication decay in equities; 2-minute-late cluster copying loses 41 %); the tools are sliding-window/discounted bandits and Bayesian online change-point detection applied to each style bucket's follower return, with the rotation clock set in copies observed rather than calendar weeks.

### Cited Findings
- pump.fun pooled graduation 0.198 % (May–June 2026) vs 0.63 % (Sept–Oct 2025), "a 3.18x decline" — [Kamat, arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02823), [Marino et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.14860); CoinGecko profitable-wallet share 30.1 % (June 2025) to 73.3 % (April 2026) — [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/solana-based-meme-coin-launchpad-pump-fun-traders-see-turnaround-in-2026-coingecko/).
- Internal: the population median of per-wallet follower medians was −15.5 (09-16), −18.2 (09-10), −19.5 (09-11), −19.0 (07-26), −18.0 (07-27); activity halved between the two halves of the 09-16 tape, so persistence figures include a session change; high-frequency is "a session effect, not a trait" (κ 0.39) — internal, measured, [shared/walletScore.ts](../../shared/walletScore.ts), [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md).
- Alpha decay: 26 % out-of-sample and 58 % post-publication decline across 97 predictors — [McLean & Pontiff](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf); measured alpha differs from true alpha by about 1.4 %/yr under decay, with a correction formula — [Understanding Alpha Decay, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4353); a 2025 preprint models decay as departure of trailing Sharpe from a fitted curve and trades on it — [arXiv 2512.11913](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11913).
- Copying late loses: 2 min behind clusters −41 % per coin (median −95 %); even positive-record groups −11 % — [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2297445). Internal: convergence worsens results monotonically; shadow wallets are the top negative-filter hypothesis — internal, [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md), [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md §5](../../docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md).
- Non-stationary bandits: with rewards constant over epochs and changing at unknown instants, Discounted-UCB and Sliding-Window-UCB (local average over the last τ plays) match the lower bound up to a log factor; any stationary policy with logarithmic regret cannot beat order T/log T under breakpoints; SW-UCB slightly better than D-UCB — [Garivier & Moulines, arXiv 0805.3415](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.3415).
- Bayesian online change-point detection tracks the run-length distribution since the last change by message passing and gives P(run length = 0) as the live change probability; it needs evidence to accumulate so it lags — [Adams & MacKay, arXiv 0710.3742](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742); score-driven BOCPD for order-flow regimes with within-regime autocorrelation — [Tsaknaki, Lillo & Mazzarisi, arXiv 2307.02375](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02375v2), [arXiv 2407.16376](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16376).
- Fund persistence vanished in 1994–2018 after holding in 1963–1993 — [Choi & Zhao](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26707.pdf).
- Internal: a top Scout pick was followed for 14 days (`SCOUT_PICK_KEEP_DAYS`) with picks every board refresh — internal, [shared/walletScout.ts](../../shared/walletScout.ts).

### Inferences
- **Rotation clock in copies, not days.** The internal reliability curve flattens at 8–10 trips and a wallet's median needs ~80+ copies to be its own evidence (Q3). A wallet producing 50 copies/day exhausts a 2-week window's information in days; one producing 5/week never accumulates it. SW-UCB/LCB with a window of the last W copies (W ≈ 100–200, to be tuned by CPCV) per wallet, instead of a fixed 7- or 14-day window, makes rotation speed proportional to activity. "Find a new wallet every week" is then a special case; the data should set the speed.
- **Style-bucket allocation as the regime object.** Because style is a fixed trait and outcomes rotate, run BOCPD not on individual wallets but on each style bucket's weekly (or daily) pooled follower median (e.g. 3 hold terciles × 3 size terciles × curve/PumpSwap entry = 18 series). A bucket whose P(change) spikes gets its wallets' priors reset (τ widened, window shortened); its weight in the top-N is reduced until its run length rebuilds. This is "meta rotation" as a measurable thing.
- **Decay features**: for each candidate, the slope of its shrunk median over the last 4 windows, Δ(shadow-wallet count), and Δ(entry gap: first print after the leader vs leader price). Internal convergence evidence predicts all three go bad together when a wallet gets crowded; the Scout itself publicising a wallet is a crowding mechanism, so picks should be monitored for post-pick decay (Q7) and the board should expect its own alpha to decay.
- **Expect base-rate drift to dominate**: a 4-point swing in the population median across tapes is as large as the best selection's edge over baseline (+5.2 points). Every weekly comparison must be against the same-week baseline, and the prior μ must be re-estimated weekly (Q4).
- **Regime-conditional priors**: at minimum two regimes (hot: population median > −12; cold: < −18) with separate k, z, and feature weights, selected by the previous week's measured population median; a hidden-Markov or BOCPD version is the upgrade.

### Gaps
- No published allocation rule that feeds BOCPD regime probabilities into position weights was found; the bucket-weighting rule above is a suggestion.
- No evidence on the optimal re-selection frequency for copy-trading leaders in any market; the MIT thesis notes follower "foraging" across leaders diversifies but does not report an optimal frequency.
- The repo has no multi-week series yet on which BOCPD or SW-UCB could be calibrated.

## Key question 6 — Model classes: learning-to-rank vs regression vs classification; monotone GBMs; what practitioners use to avoid lucky streaks

### Takeaway
With a few hundred eligible wallets per week and at most tens of weeks of labels, the evidence-backed order is: empirical-Bayes shrinkage with a lower bound first (it already beats every raw statistic internally and is what fund-selection research converged on), a small monotone-constrained GBM on shrunk features second (LightGBM supports per-feature ±1 constraints with no stated objective restriction), and learning-to-rank only once there are enough weekly query groups, because LambdaMART's own evidence is from equity cross-sections with thousands of groups; practitioners in tipster and fund selection avoid lucky streaks by demanding sample sizes in the thousands, deflating for the number of candidates examined, and weighting loser-avoidance over winner-picking.

### Cited Findings
- LightGBM `monotone_constraints`: per-feature 1/−1/0; `monotone_constraints_method` basic (fast, over-constrains), intermediate ("much less constraining"), advanced ("even less constraining"); `monotone_penalty` forbids monotone splits on the first X levels; the docs list no objective restriction, but categorical features cannot be constrained and with `linear_tree` constraints apply only to split selection; `lambdarank` uses integer labels with `label_gain` (default 0,1,3,7,15,…), `rank_xendcg` is faster with similar performance — [LightGBM Parameters](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Parameters.html). LightGBM's built-in method over-constrains trees and better methods exist — [arXiv 2011.00986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00986v1).
- Learning-to-rank for cross-sectional strategies: regression/classification outputs sorted are "suboptimal" for ordering; ~3× Sharpe over traditional cross-sectional momentum (equities, preprint) — [Poh et al., arXiv 2012.07149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07149); LambdaMART + SHAP outperformed regression and classification for Shanghai A-share selection — [Aston](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/a-novel-interpretable-stock-selection-algorithm-for-quantitative-); a benchmark of pointwise/pairwise/listwise losses for stock ranking — [arXiv 2510.14156](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14156v1); RankGLU argues cross-sectional prediction is closer to ranking than magnitude regression — [arXiv 2606.08930](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08930).
- Memecoin-specific: XGBoost had the highest AUC among LASSO/XGBoost/MLP (exact values only in figures) and all three gave negative copier returns — [arXiv 2601.08641v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2).
- White Whale's neural network raised the doubling rate from ~10 % to 26 % yet lost every day — [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2297445).
- Fund selection converged on EB/random-effects shrinkage — [Harvey & Liu](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v31y2018i7p2499-2552..html); loser persistence is the robust part — [Berk & Tonks](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13042.pdf), [Choi & Zhao](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26707.pdf).
- Tipster selection: > 10,000 bets at > 9 % net to be called expert at 5 % (search-snippet attribution) — [J. Gambling Studies](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2Fs10899-013-9420-7.pdf); systematic review: skill plays no significant role in nearly all studies — [UKE review](https://fis.uke.de/portal/en/publications/sportwetten-expertise-oder-gluck-ein-systematischer-review-uber-tippstudien(f5e1e59c-3b17-4b89-b5c6-24bb9320ab9e).html).
- Multiple-testing hurdles: t > 3 for new factors — [Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592); DSR and PBO — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551), [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253).
- Internal: the Copy score (a weighted mix of follower return, follower win, reachable, consistency, focus) "adds nothing over follower return + follower win" (ρ 0.47 vs 0.48) and its top decile did worse than the shrunk median alone (−6.5 vs −4.7) — internal, measured, [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md).

### Inferences
- **Stage 0 (ship now)**: EB-shrunk median with weekly-estimated (μ, k) and an LCB (Q4). Everything else must beat this in paired walk-forward.
- **Stage 1**: monotone-constrained LightGBM regression on the shrunk median (or on the 3-grade label) with ≤ 10 features, each given a sign from Q1 (reachable % +, creator −, large size −, shadow count −, slope of shrunk median +, recent drawdown −, dead-coin share −, hold-time stability +), `monotone_constraints_method = advanced`, depth ≤ 3, strong L2, ≥ 50 min_data_in_leaf, trained on wallet-weeks with the feature window ending before the label week. Monotonicity is the regularisation that keeps a 300-row-per-week model from learning tape idiosyncrasies. Predictions feed the same LCB rule.
- **Stage 2 (only with ≥ 30 weekly groups)**: `lambdarank`/`rank_xendcg` with one query group per week and the 3-grade label; evaluate NDCG@10 against the shrunk-median baseline. Because the docs do not restrict monotone constraints by objective but also do not confirm they interact correctly with the pairwise lambda gradients, test monotonicity empirically (partial-dependence sign checks) before trusting it.
- **Classification vs regression**: the P(week median > fee floor) classifier is the calibration-friendly head (Q7) and the natural "recommend nobody" switch (no wallet above 0.5 → empty list). Regression on the median is for display. Both heads can share the GBM.
- **Avoiding lucky streaks the way practitioners do**: (a) hard sample floors that scale with dispersion (Q3's ~80 copies for "reliable"); (b) deflate for the number of wallets examined — with 263 candidates, the expected maximum of 263 noisy medians is well above the population even with no skill, so the top-1's raw median must be compared with the order-statistic null, not with zero; (c) prefer dropping the persistent bottom (bottom decile −38 %, persistent across periods) to picking the top (decile 10 < decile 9); (d) report every ranker's PBO over the variants tried.

### Gaps
- No LTR application to trader/leader selection was found in any market; the equity LTR results are not a transfer guarantee.
- Whether LightGBM monotone constraints are honoured under `lambdarank` is not stated in the docs.
- The Blogabet 1.5 M-tip study (tipsters won 3.29 % more than implied, 3.97 % yield) surfaced only as a snippet without a confirmed URL; its selection on self-posting tipsters is likely severe.

## Key question 7 — Scout self-scoring: metrics and dashboards that make a wallet finder's own skill measurable week over week

### Takeaway
The board already records picks-vs-everyone (top-10, 14-day follow, ≥ 20 copies each side); to make skill measurable it needs the controls the internal protocol already names (random buys hold-matched and curvePct-matched, the leader-PnL top-N as the "pump.fun leaderboard" proxy, decile 9 vs 10), per-week paired differences with CIs rather than a pooled total, calibration of predicted vs realised shrunk medians (slope < 1 means under-shrinkage), and a fast-converging process metric analogous to betting's closing-line value, since outcome metrics at these dispersions need months to separate skill from luck.

### Cited Findings
- Internal board record: `SCOUT_PICKS_N = 10`, `SCOUT_PICK_KEEP_DAYS = 14`, `SCOUT_RECORD_MIN_COPIES = 20`; a pick stores `shrunkPct` and `fTripsAtPick` at pick time and the follower returns of trips the wallet *opened after* the pick; the record reports pooled median and win rate of picks vs every priced copy opened since the oldest pick, both repriced at the user's size, with `enough` requiring ≥ 20 copies on both sides — internal, [shared/walletScout.ts](../../shared/walletScout.ts) (`ScoutPick`, `BoardRecord`, `boardRecord`).
- Internal controls already measured: random buy held as long as the selection's own copied trips −8.5 % (vs −9.9 % average wallet mirror, i.e. mirroring the average wallet is 1.4 points worse than random with the same hold); leader-PnL top-N −6.52/−10.99 % vs follower-realisable −3.42/−4.11 %; decile 9 vs 10 — internal, measured, [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](../../docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md), [docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md addendum](../../docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md).
- Internal: "letting the board recommend nobody, rather than its least bad wallet" is listed as what would help the product — internal, [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](../../docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md).
- Closing-line value in betting is used as a skill proxy because "a bettor can keep beating the closing line while losing bets", separating skill from luck in a way win rate cannot (opinion piece) — [SimpleFunctions](https://simplefunctions.dev/concepts/from-sports-betting-clv-to-pm-trade-quality); Pinnacle-referenced CLV is the predictive series, CLV against softer references is structurally negative (vendor doc) — [SharpAPI](https://docs.sharpapi.io/en/api-reference/historical-clv/); a tipster platform evaluates ROI and CLV "over hundreds of bets, not short periods" (marketing) — [Bet2Invest profile](https://sk.linkedin.com/in/filipfischer).
- PBO: share of splits where the in-sample winner is bottom-half out of sample — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); DSR for a selected series — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551).
- Winner's-curse CIs on selected winners are invalid without correction — [arXiv 2603.12867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12867).

### Inferences
**Weekly scorecard (one row per calendar week, paired on the same week and universe):**
1. Top-N realised: pooled median, 5 %-winsorised mean, win rate, total SOL at reference size, number of copies, number of picks that traded.
2. Controls on the same week: (a) universe baseline (every eligible wallet's copies); (b) random-N wallets from the universe (re-drawn 200×, report the 5th–95th percentile band — the top-N must sit above the band, which is the order-statistic null from Q6); (c) random buys matched on curvePct, hour and the picks' hold distribution; (d) leader-PnL top-N (the pump.fun-leaderboard proxy); (e) decile-9 selection; (f) last week's top-N held another week (rotation value).
3. Paired differences vs each control with a wallet-clustered bootstrap CI; a running paired t over weeks with the Q3 table deciding when the gap is resolved.
4. Hit rate: share of picks whose realised week median beats the universe median; share beating the fee floor; share "silent" (no copies).
5. Calibration: scatter and reliability bins of predicted shrunk median (at pick) vs realised median; fit realised = a + b·predicted across picks; b < 1 means the ranker is under-shrunk (raise k), b ≈ 1 with a < 0 means a stale prior (lower μ). For the classifier head, Brier score and a reliability diagram of P(median > fee floor).
6. Rank quality: Spearman between pick-time score and realised median over the whole universe (not just the top-N) — this is the week's ρ, with the half-day 0.48 as the reference; NDCG@10 with the 3-grade label.
7. Decay tracking: realised median of picks by days-since-pick (0–2, 3–7, 8–14) and by Δ(shadow count) since pick; a monotone fall is the board's own alpha decay.
8. Process metric (the CLV analogue): for every follower fill, the mark 30 s and 5 min after the fill vs the fill price, before any exit — a selection whose picks' fills are immediately under water is paying for crowding regardless of outcome; it converges in hundreds of fills rather than tens of weeks. Report the median instant mark per week for picks vs universe.
9. Honesty switches on the dashboard: show "recommend nobody" when the classifier's best P(median > fee floor) is below 0.5 or the conformal set is empty; show "not enough weeks" until the paired t has the weeks the Q3 table requires for the observed σ_d; show PBO of the current ranker over its variant grid.

**Dashboard shape**: a per-week table (rows = weeks, columns = top-N and each control, cells = median ± CI), a cumulative paired-difference line with its CI fan, the calibration scatter, the decay-by-age bars, and the instant-mark distribution. Pool across weeks only as medians of weekly medians, never as a grand pooled copy median (a single hyperactive pick would otherwise dominate, as 5Voxqwa8's 2,160 trips would).

### Gaps
- No empirical study comparing the convergence speed of CLV vs ROI was found; the instant-mark metric's variance advantage is an inference, to be verified on the repo's own fills.
- No published copy-trading product or study reports a self-scoring protocol of this kind; the controls come from the internal protocol and fund/betting analogies.
- The board's current record pools copies across picks and across the whole follow span; it does not yet produce per-week rows, so the paired tests above cannot be computed from existing data without a schema change (not made; read-only).

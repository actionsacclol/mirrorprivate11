# Build the Scout That Can Recommend Nobody

On every measurement that exists, no wallet on the pump.fun curve is profitable to copy from a desktop follower about two seconds behind. Across **9.3 million curve trades, three independent day-pairs and 630 decile × lag × fill-model cells, zero cells have a median copy return at or above zero**; the best train-selected decile tests at **−7.5 to −9.2 % per copy at 0.1 SOL against a fee floor of −7.60 %**, and all **12 of 12** Scout wallets that read positive on a 30-trip sample were negative over a chain-priced full week ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §1, §4.1](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md); [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)). The realistic ceiling is therefore "the best wallets we can find lose about the fees; everyone else loses more": of a roughly 18-point pooled loss, a better scout can only touch the 1–7 point adverse-selection slice, because fees, fixed costs, the leader's own price impact and the lag increment are structural. "Needing a new wallet every week" is not a property of the market but the winner's curse observed weekly: a 30-trip median carries a standard error near 9 points, so the top of a 263-wallet list reads 15–35 points above its true value and reverts the week after it is picked. No competitor measures what a follower would have made; every leaderboard surveyed ranks the leader's own PnL, a statistic the internal tape shows is worse than random as a copy signal. The route to being the best copy-trading software in the world is therefore not to find the positive wallet nobody has demonstrated exists, but to be the only product whose numbers are the follower's own, chain-complete, controlled against random and leaderboard baselines, and honest enough to recommend nobody, while running the single pre-registered test that could still find an edge: PumpSwap/Raydium-stage leaders, where fee and impact are mechanically lower and no tape has ever been run. The plan below builds that instrument in priority order and ends with an 8-week test whose outcome decides whether Krypto Copy ships as a copier or as a vetting tool.

## Every measurement puts the ceiling at the fee floor, not above zero

The strongest evidence is the team's own, and it is unusually complete. The lag replay priced copies with an honest fill model (STATE-F: trade against the curve state after the last print strictly before the due time, skip trips the leader closed before the follower could buy, settle dead coins at their last print, mirror sell fractions, charge 1.25 % + 0.5 % per side plus 0.003504 SOL fixed plus the follower's own impact) and found that "no decile reaches breakeven at any lag on any pair, under any of the three fill models, at 0.05 or 0.1 SOL"; a copied round trip with no price move costs **−10.79 % at 0.05 SOL and −7.60 % at 0.1 SOL** ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §1, §2.2, §4.1](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)). An adversarial re-run with a fresh bootstrap reproduced the point estimates to the second decimal and verified the fee arithmetic against the live cost code in `shared/exitBudget.ts` and `shared/copytrade.ts`: "these are real live costs on the fast lane, not paper inventions" ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md §2](./swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md)). The earlier two-period replay with a gentler cost model (1.5 %/side, no fixed fees) reached the same sign: the least-bad decile sat at **−1.2 %/−1.3 %** and its gross follower return before any fee was **−2.6 %/−3.3 %**, so "deleting our fee entirely leaves it negative" ([docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](./wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md)). The full-week chain-priced check of the twelve best Scout wallets on 2026-10-24 closed the loop at the level of individual wallets: the one that read **+0.81 SOL on 30 trips was −9.80 SOL over 2,027 priced trips**, negative on every one of five days, with a median copy of −22.5 % and a mean of −4.8 % that depended on ten copies of up to +1,461 %; without those ten it was −16.4 SOL ([docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)).

Nothing outside the repo contradicts this. The one published positive number, **+3 % per coin for a copier**, comes from a WWW'26 paper whose copier fills immediately at the post-leader reserve with zero latency, fee treatment unstated and no slippage value; its own ablations without the wallet-history or coin agent give copier gross returns of 0.933 and 0.942, which are losses ([arXiv:2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08641); [docs/swarm-2026-10-23/external.md §5](./swarm-2026-10-23/external.md)). White Whale Labs' audit of 384 million swaps across 7.7 million wallets found that backtesting a single wallet **lost 20 % per transaction**, entering two minutes behind large clusters lost **41 % per coin (n = 93, median −95 %)**, following clusters with positive records still lost 11 %, and concluded there was "no monetizable edge" ([ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2297445)). The cleanest external analogue in a liquid market is eToro, where **mirrored trades averaged −61.24 bps against −6.88 bps for the same users' own trades**, and mirror traders lost −10.98 bps per trade while non-mirror traders made +1.15 ([arXiv:2507.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01817)). On Fomo, the Solana social-trading app, **6.16 % of 292,000 wallets finished three months in profit** ([Yellow.com](https://yellow.com/news/fomo-copy-trading-94-percent-wallet-losses)). The vendors' own models are the outlier: GMGN's copy-tradeability backtest assumes the price drifts only 1.5 % per second of lag, or 1.35–4.5 points at its 3 s default, where the team measured **about 26 % of price gone between a leader's print and the next print a follower could fill at** ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/external.md §4](./swarm-2026-10-23/external.md); [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md §4](./best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md)).

Where the loss comes from matters more than its size, because it tells the scout what it can and cannot fix. At 0.1 SOL and 2 s under the honest model the pooled median is −15.6 to −20.4 % across six day files ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §3](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)), and the skeptic's component arithmetic decomposes a representative −18 % as follows ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md §2 L2](./swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md); [docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §1.3, §3, §4.2](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)):

| Component at 0.1 SOL, 2 s, STATE-F | Points | Can a scout reduce it? |
|---|---|---|
| Venue + Krypt fees at 1.75 %/side | ≈ 3.5 | No; needs a venue or size change |
| Fixed 0.003504 SOL per round trip | ≈ 3.5 | No; falls only with larger copies |
| Follower's own impact at 30 SOL depth | ≈ 0.7 | Only via size |
| Lag increment from 0.4 s to 2 s | 1.1–1.6 | Not from a desktop; a $49/mo prebuild buys 0.3–1.1 |
| Dead-coin accounting on the pooled median | 0.3–0.4 | Partly, through rug filters |
| Leader's impact + adverse selection + coin bleed over the hold | ≈ 8–11 | Only the adverse-selection slice, worth 1–7 |

The random control pins the scout's share. A random buy matched on UTC hour, curve band and the trip's exact exit schedule lands at **−7.8 to −12.0 % median**; every ranked wallet pool is 1–7 points worse than that control, and the top decile is **−0.9 to +3.3 points relative to its control**, which is why the lag note concludes that "the shrunk median works by avoiding wallets that are worse than a random buy, not by finding ones that beat it" ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §4.2](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)). The skeptic weakened this to "true for medians only": by 1 %-trimmed mean the top decile beats its control by 4.4 points at 0.4 s and 8.3 at 2 s on the 09-16 pair ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md §2 L3](./swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md)), the one measured hint that selection buys something in the right tail even though it never moves the typical copy above the floor. The gap between "all ranked" (−13 to −16 %) and "top decile" (−7.8 to −9.2 %) is about 5–7 points and ends exactly at the fee floor; there is no measured population whose copies move price in the follower's favour by more than that floor.

Speed does not rescue it either, and the lag curve is the single strongest argument against spending on it. Under the honest model the pooled median is −14.5 to −19.1 % at 0.4 s, −15.6 to −20.4 % at 2 s, −16.9 to −24.9 % at 30 s and −17.2 to −32.9 % at 120 s, while the top decile moves by less than a point between 0.4 s and 2 s (−7.80 → −8.19 in September, −8.52 → −9.20 in July); the pre-registered speed hypotheses P2 and P3 failed and the $49/mo prebuild "never reaches 0" ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §3, §4.1, §7](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)). Interpolating that curve, even an impossible 0 s desktop fill would leave the top decile around the floor and the population around −14 to −18 %. The structural reason is the leaders themselves: the median leader hold is **six seconds**, mirrored both-leg positions have a median hold of 0.1 minutes, "the leaders' edge is latency" ([docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](./wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md)), and the least-bad decile's leaders have a median first buy of 0.010–0.034 SOL, so a 0.1 SOL follower is 3–20× their size and 14–24 % of their trips are exact no-move round trips ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §4.1](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)). Slot time is now 211–216 ms after Alpenglow, so 2 s is about nine slots, and same-slot fills are "not reachable from a desktop" ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/external.md §1.1](./swarm-2026-10-23/external.md)).

Three gaps keep this from being the final word, and the design below is built to close them. No tape covers PumpSwap or Raydium-stage leaders, so the ceiling for post-migration copying is unmeasured; the tape predates the 200 ms slot regime (316.8 ms/slot on 09-16); and 2–6.5 % of pump rows were undecodable V3 instructions that hide some leader sells in a direction nobody knows ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §7](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)). Regime is probably the largest unmeasured covariate: CoinGecko's realised-PnL series has the share of profitable pump.fun wallets swinging from **30.1 % in June 2025 to 73.3 % in April 2026** ([CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)), although Bitquery could not reproduce the April figure and got 27 % with CoinGecko's netting on its own data ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money)). A copy result measured in one regime month says little about another, which is why every test in this report runs a same-week control.

## "A new wallet every week" is the winner's curse observed weekly

The internal tape separates cleanly what persists from what does not. Style is nearly a fixed trait: split-half Spearman correlations on the 09-16 tape are **0.94–0.98 for median first-buy size, 0.84–0.93 for the share of trips under 60 s and 0.83–0.90 for median hold**. Outcomes persist only moderately: the follower median return correlates **0.48–0.49** between halves, own return falls from 0.37 to 0.28 as trip counts rise, and split-half reliability of the follower median climbs from 0.31 at 3 trips to 0.55 at 40 and never reaches 0.7 ([docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)). Training-day deciles do carry to the test day in order in all three periods, so followability is a property not pure noise, but what persists is "least bad near the fee floor", and the extreme top misbehaves: **decile 9 beat decile 10 in three separate periods**, which the convergence addendum reads as "the extreme top of the ranking is fitted to the training day" ([docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](./wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md); [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)). On the 2026-10-24 board, **15 of 263 eligible wallets read above zero, "about what luck alone produces in a sample that size"**, and the Scout record itself "leans toward its coins that survived": one wallet-day held 10 recorded trips where the chain replay found 23, the extras mostly −85 to −100 % dead coins ([docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)).

The arithmetic of why the weekly hunt feels productive is simple. With per-copy returns having roughly 1 % of their mass per point near the median (an IQR near 50 points, consistent with 30-trip medians ranging from −85 % to +1 % on the 10-24 shortlist), the asymptotic standard error of a median, 1/(2·f(m)·√n) ([Wikipedia: Median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median)), is about 9 points at 30 trips and 16 at 10. The expected maximum of 263 such noisy estimates sits about √(2 ln 263) ≈ 3.3 standard errors above the mean, so **the top wallet on any week's board reads roughly 15–35 points above its true value**; a wallet whose true median is the population's −15.5 % reads around 0 to +20 % the week it is picked and about −15 % the next, and that regression, seen from inside, is "we need a new wallet" ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md Q6](./swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md); the arithmetic is an estimate built on the anchors in [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)). Under the shrunk median with k = 10 and a −15.5 % prior, pure noise predicts 2–10 % of wallets above zero depending on dispersion; the observed 5.7 % is inside that band, and 12 of 12 reverting on the full week is the empirical confirmation.

Every adjacent market with a real test shows the same shape. In online poker, with 456 million hands, the top decile by raw win rate stayed in the top decile **13.6 % of the time against a 10 % no-persistence base**, rising to 20.7 % when players were ranked by a t-statistic instead; player-level Spearman between halves was 0.07–0.15 ([Potter van Loon et al., PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0115479)). On eToro, 30-day rolling performance has an autocorrelation indistinguishable from a shuffled null, popularity is persistent, and the correlation between performance and popularity is 0.11 ([arXiv:2507.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01817)). On Polymarket, 44 % of accounts classified as skilled on one half of their events keep the label on the other half, versus about 10 % for active mutual funds, but only **12 % of top raw-PnL earners pass the skill test** and about 60 % of "lucky winners" revert to losses; the split is random rather than chronological, so 44 % is an upper bound on any walk-forward result ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study); [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/wisdom-of-the-few-prediction-markets-are-driven-by-small-number-of-skilled-traders)). The one high persistence number in crypto, a Spearman of 0.52 between adjacent 10-day windows on Hyperliquid, ranks wallets on 10-second markout with at least 100 orders, a low-noise trait rather than a PnL, and the paper explicitly declines to model execution or copying ([arXiv:2608.04373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04373)). The one case where ranking on past returns did select a persistent top group, Taiwan day traders whose prior-year top 500 earned 37.9 bps/day net the next year, required a full year of ranking data and still found that fewer than 1 % of day traders reliably profit net of fees ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). Manager selection says the same from the other side: hired managers chosen on strong excess returns do not outperform after hiring and fired ones do as well as their replacements ([Goyal & Wahal, JF 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i4p1805-1847.html)), and recent losers beat recent winners by 2.28 percentage points a year over the usual three-year window ([Cornell, Hsu & Nanigian](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20180321-095702727)).

Leaderboards make the curse worse rather than better. On the Bybit and TraderWagon copy-trading boards, popularity correlates −0.53 to −0.65 with the platform's default rank and about **zero with win rate**; portfolios have a median life of 2.9 days, only 44 % of the 7-day-ROI top-20 are in the 30-day top-20, leaders run up to six portfolios and keep the winners, and leaders on the leaderboard page earn half to three quarters of their profit from copier commissions ([Kawai et al., CHI 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf)). On an LLM-agent vault leaderboard, a rendered top-3 cut produced a **1.75× discontinuity in entry rate**, i.e. display position itself routes capital ([arXiv:2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)). The internal evidence that leader-PnL ranking is worse than the baseline (top decile by own return −14.3 % against a −9.9 % baseline) is the same mechanism measured on pump.fun ([docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)).

The implication for cadence is sharp. Weekly re-selection is the right rhythm for a style and least-bad filter, because styles persist day to day and harmful wallets stay harmful, and the wrong rhythm for finding a positive wallet, because no positive class has been measured to persist and, if one existed, Barber's result says it would take months and hundreds of trips to identify. It also sets the statistical budget: a top-10 paper follow produces roughly 300 copies a week, which can detect a true median of about +7 points in one week, 3.6 in four weeks and 2.5 in eight; proving a positive mean weekly P&L at the 95 % lower bound takes about 8 weeks if the true edge is +5 % per copy and about 50 weeks if it is +2 %, because the per-copy standard deviation is on the order of 100 points ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md Q7](./swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md)). Multiplicity is the other budget: the lag study expected about two one-sided false positives among its 90 cells by chance alone ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §0](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)), the finance literature asks a newly claimed effect to clear t ≈ 3.0 rather than 2.0 once the number of tests is counted ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)), and "under memory effects, backtest overfitting leads to negative expected returns out-of-sample, rather than zero" ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/)). The tape is not the limiter; 9.3 million trades already resolve deciles to ±0.2–0.6 points. The effect is.

## The field ranks leaders; nobody measures the follower, and that is the opening

Across sixteen products surveyed internally, none publishes a follower-side outcome or a checkable copy latency, and all rank leaders by their own PnL ([docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md §2](./best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md)). The vendors' own documentation confirms it in their own words. GMGN defines **"Smart Money: Wallet who often earns money"** and its Discover columns are the wallet's own 1D/7D/30D PnL and 7-day win rate, with no numeric threshold for any label ([GMGN icon definitions](https://docs.gmgn.ai/index/featured-icon-definition.md)). Kolscan, now owned by pump.fun, ranks by SOL gain with no methodology page and no sample-size control: on 2026-10-25 its #1 row showed **"12 / 0" for +3,825 SOL while #4 showed "57 / 77" for +86 SOL**, a 12-trade record outranking a 134-trade one, and admission is by self-application with "$100k+ PnL in recent months" ([kolscan.io/leaderboard](https://kolscan.io/leaderboard); [kolscan.io](https://kolscan.io)). Solana Tracker's top-traders endpoint defaults to `minTrades=20`, `minDays=3` and `excludeArbitrage=true`, sorts by realised PnL, and its own docs say the filters "do not predict future returns"; a "Copy-Trade Performance" board is listed as coming soon ([Solana Tracker leaderboards guide](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/pnl-v2/leaderboards.md); [Solana Tracker leaderboards](https://www.solanatracker.io/leaderboard)). Cielo ranks Discovery by realised PnL over 1/7/30 days and publishes the field's only numeric label thresholds, High Win Rate above 75 %, Gem Finder at 30 % of tokens reaching 2×, and Sniper at average hold under two minutes with the warning that "manual copying is unlikely to match the results" ([Cielo Wallet Discovery](https://docs.cielo.finance/wallet-tracking/discovery/wallet-discovery.md); [Cielo Finding Good Wallets](https://docs.cielo.finance/guides/copy-trading/finding-good-wallets.md)). Nansen's Smart Trader is a rank label, "top-ranked wallets by PnL in past 30 days", and the existence of a "Former Smart Trader" label is a built-in admission that the ranking does not persist ([Nansen Labels 101](https://academy.nansen.ai/articles/2149924-labels-and-watchlists-101)). Trojan's own copy-trading docs concede that "good copy-trading wallets are rarely shared publicly" and that "snipers are a poor fit because, by the time they copy, you land several blocks behind their entry" ([Trojan Copy Trading](https://docs.trojan.com/terminal-overview/tools-and-widgets/copy-trading-bot.md); [Trojan FAQs](https://docs.trojan.com/faqs.md)).

Only two small 2026 entrants even claim a follower-side number. KOL Brain publishes a "copyability" score as "how much of a wallet's edge survives being followed with about 3 seconds of latency and about 5 % slippage", uses the median of first buy to first sell rather than the average, shrinks win rate toward the pooled rate with a pseudo-count of 20, and labels wallets with fewer than 20 trades "Unrated"; but its constants are fixed rather than measured and it still keys off leader profit per position ([KOL Brain methodology](https://kolbrain.fun/methodology)). Wallet Master claims its metrics "count only trades you could have copied yourself, and fees and tips are deducted", with the method undisclosed behind a $250/mo API ([docs.walletmaster.tools](https://docs.walletmaster.tools)). MadeOnSol filters 952,643 candidate wallets to roughly four hundred on `win_rate >= 0.6` and `tokens_traded >= 30`, publishes its bot classifier, and explicitly does not claim copying them is profitable, warning that "following bots makes you their exit liquidity"; it reports no out-of-sample test ([MadeOnSol methodology](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-alpha-wallets-methodology-952k-dataset)). The academic case for the follower-side metric is already written: on eToro, reweighting selection from popularity toward performance by 10 % raises modelled platform performance 6.6 %, and "explorers" who revise whom they mirror outperform static "keepers" ([arXiv:2507.01817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01817)); on social-trading platforms generally, "choosing the traders with the highest accumulated returns leads to large losses" while risk-adjusted rankings do better, and activity is negatively related to returns ([Dorfleitner et al., QREF 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v70y2018icp160-171.html)).

That is the defensible product sentence, and it is already measured: "We show you what *you* would have made copying this wallet, after our fees and yours, and we will tell you when the answer is nobody." Ranking by follower-realisable return instead of leader PnL **halves the loss (−3.42 %/−4.11 % versus −6.52 %/−10.99 %) and lifts win rate ten points in both periods**, and the STATE-F ranking beats the app's current 2 s DROP ranking by 1.3–3.2 points on every pair ([docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](./wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md); [docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §4.1](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md)). The evidence also rules several things out as defaults. Convergence is a negative signal, not a confirmation: when K followed top wallets buy the same mint within five minutes the follower's 60-minute median falls monotonically from **−14.9 % at K = 1 to −32.1 % at K = 4**, with 8,016 four-wallet events in a single day ([docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](./wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md)). Slow leaders test worse at the median (the >60-minute hold bucket is −17.11 %/−2.28 %) and their positive trimmed means of +9.0/+7.7 on 194/452 positions are "two big winners, not an edge, and it is exactly the shape this project has twice mistaken for a signal" ([docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](./wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md)). Long holders (κ 0.65, −14.6 vs −9.7), large-size leaders (κ 0.80, −13.6 vs −9.8) and coin creators (κ 1.00, −16.2 vs −9.5) are stable labels with measured harm and belong on warning chips ([docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)).

One growth hypothesis survives, and it survives only because nobody has measured it. Copying PumpSwap or Raydium-stage leaders is the single condition with a mechanical reason to be better: PumpSwap's tiered fee falls from 1.25 % to 1.00 % above roughly 4,420 SOL market cap, impact in a deep pool is near zero, dead coins are rarer after graduation, and holds are longer so nine slots of lag matter less; the floor at 0.1 SOL would still be about −6.8 %, which the price move must beat ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/external.md §3.3](./swarm-2026-10-23/external.md); [docs/swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md Q1, Q5](./swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md)). All existing evidence is "curve-only: no PumpSwap or other AMM leaders", and the 10-24 chain replay now prices PumpSwap legs at executed price at about five legs per second, so the measurement is finally cheap ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §7](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md); [docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)). It should be sold internally as a measurement, not a feature, until the test in the final section reports.

## The scout, built in priority order

The design that follows treats the scout as an instrument whose first job is to measure its own skill honestly, because the measured gap between a good ranking and the fee floor is small and the gap between a lucky board and a good one is invisible without controls. Priorities are ordered by what the evidence says changes outcomes, by cost, and by what must exist before the pre-registered test can run.

| Priority | Component | What to build | Why (evidence) | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0, this week | Data honesty | Chain-complete labels for every ranked wallet or a 20 % random sample of the universe; coverage % and dead-coin share of unseen trips on every row; fee floor shown beside every follower number; board allowed to recommend nobody; per-week record schema | 10 recorded vs 23 chain trips; 15/263 ≈ luck; floor −7.6 %/−10.8 % | $0 (Helius Free, ~350 credits/wallet) |
| P0 | Hard exclusions | Creator of any coin; buy in create tx, creator-paid fee or index +1..+4 in the creation slot; shared signer/fee payer; same-transaction round trips (WT1); sniper share; bot rate ≥ 40/min; transferred-in tokens priced as unknown cost | 185/186 bundle validation; devs sell within a minute on 65 % of launches; 55 % of funded snipes exit < 60 s | $0 |
| P0 | Execution hygiene | Slippage default out of the 10–20 % band; fee-aware mirroring (ignore sell slices < 50 %); 30-min wall-clock backstop; `jitodontfront` added but not marketed | 87 vs 8 sandwiches per 10k; +0.5 to +1.7 pts | $0 |
| P1, weeks 1–8 | Ranking core | Empirical-Bayes shrunk median with weekly-estimated prior μ and k = σ²/τ²; rank on a lower confidence bound; hierarchical prior by style bucket; rank only at ≥ 10 trips, "reliable" at ~80 copies | k = 10 is EB in disguise; prior moved −15.5 to −19.5 across tapes; decile 9 > decile 10 | $0 |
| P1 | The test | 8-week pre-registered paper follow with TOP, RAND, LEAD, FLOOR arms, 0–9 slot ablation, rank-decay track, separate PumpSwap arm | Section below | ≤ $49/mo; ~60k credits |

### Data: chain-complete or nothing

The Scout's record is a candidate generator, not a label source, because it only sees trades its live feed carried and therefore over-ranks wallets whose coins survived ([docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)). Labels must come from the chain-slot pricer: one Helius `getTransactionsForAddress` call on the mint with `filters.slot.lte = leader slot + 9`, price from the last pump `TradeEvent` reserves or PumpSwap executed amounts at or before that slot. Measured against the swap API on 26 trips the median difference was **0.0 points**, 16 of 26 within 10 points; pricing by the second rather than the slot was off by ±70 points on single trips because a pump coin "can move hard within one second" ([docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md); [docs/engineering-2026-10-24.md #10](./engineering-2026-10-24.md); [electron/engine/chainPrice.ts](../electron/engine/chainPrice.ts)). At 10 credits per 100 transactions ([Helius credits](https://www.helius.dev/docs/billing/credits)), 30 trips price in 2.75 s for 320 credits and a prototype did 4,320 legs in 14 minutes, so a fully follower-scored wallet costs about 350 credits: roughly **650 wallets a week on the free 1M-credit plan, 5–6k a week on the $49 Developer plan** ([docs/engineering-2026-10-24.md #10–#11](./engineering-2026-10-24.md); [Helius plans](https://www.helius.dev/docs/billing/plans)). If chain labels cannot cover the whole universe, label a random 20 % of the non-shortlisted wallets each week so the baseline is unbiased and the shortlist's coverage bias can be estimated, and weight each recorded trip by 1/coverage of its wallet-day. Open trips at a week boundary are marked at the boundary print; dead coins settle at the last mark with a −100 % sensitivity reported, never dropped.

For live detection, Helius Parsed Streams on a free key delivered every one of 5,630 transactions that `logsSubscribe` did, a median **119 ms earlier**, at one credit per event ([docs/engineering-2026-10-24.md #9](./engineering-2026-10-24.md); [Helius Parsed Streams](https://www.helius.dev/docs/parsed-streams)). Whole-market coverage is a different animal: pump.fun plus PumpSwap run on the order of **25 million trade events a day** (a 12-hour mainnet soak of an open-source Yellowstone indexer processed 12.4 million trades at 29–36 MB of memory) and about **10 GB/h of gRPC traffic**, which makes metered plans the expensive route and a residential desktop the wrong terminal ([shaurya35/solana-realtime-indexer](https://github.com/shaurya35/solana-realtime-indexer)). Flat-rate gRPC runs $150–199 a month (Shyft Build $199 with unlimited bandwidth and 150k-address filters; Corvus $160) and a small VPS indexer following the Carbon pattern would score every wallet with at least five closed trips, on the order of 10⁴ a week, with slot-exact pricing at zero marginal credit cost, for a total near **$280–330 a month** ([Shyft pricing](https://shyft.to/solana-rpc-grpc-pricing); [Corvus pricing comparison](https://docs.corvus-labs.io/platform/pricing-comparison)). Two cheaper whole-market routes exist for the ranking backtest rather than for live scoring: Dune's `dex_solana.trades` carries `block_slot`, `trader_id` and per-leg amounts, so one SQL query can compute follower-lagged returns for every wallet in a week on a $75 Analyst seat with roughly three hours of lag, if its curve coverage is verified first ([Dune Solana DEX trades](https://docs.dune.com/data-catalog/curated/dex-trades/solana/solana-dex-trades.md); [Dune pricing](https://comparedge.com/tools/dune-analytics/pricing)); and a bulk pump.fun Parquet archive of about 1.8 billion trades sells for **$200 per month of data**, pending confirmation that its trades table carries slots ([nolimitnodes](https://nolimitnodes.com/products/historic-pump-fun)). The sequencing follows the money: Free now, Developer for the test, backend only if an arm clears.

Terms matter here because a chain-only scout is also the legally cleaner one. pump.fun's Terms restrict bots that monitor its services or track other users, which covers the swap-API scans, home-feed seeds, username lookups and leaderboard reads but not the chain feed ([docs/copy-trader-swarm-2026-10-23.md §2, §6](./copy-trader-swarm-2026-10-23.md)); Helius forbids distributing or making derivative works of "Helius Products", which is why the user's own key should stay the design and a vendor backend publishing derived scores should be cleared with Helius first ([Helius terms](https://docs.helius.dev/terms)); and Solana Tracker explicitly bars competing products without written authorization while permitting application building and forbidding raw-data redistribution ([Solana Tracker terms](https://www.solanatracker.io/terms)).

### Target and signals

The target is the follower's own slot-lagged, cost-inclusive return per copy at a reference size of 0.1 SOL, summarised by the pooled per-copy median, because that is the statistic that persisted (ρ ≈ 0.48) and the one a shrinkage prior exists for. The mean is tail-owned: the 5Voxqwa8 week moved by 6.6 SOL on ten of 2,027 copies ([docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)). Log three secondaries for every wallet-week because they answer different questions: a 5 %-winsorised mean that tracks P&L without letting one +1,461 % copy decide, the probability the week's median clears the fee floor as a relevance grade, and the lower 80 % bootstrap bound of the median. Do not use a per-trade Sharpe: with 10–50 heavy-tailed copies, kurtosis dominates and the deflation needed for selection would swamp the estimate ([Bailey & López de Prado, Deflated Sharpe](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). Publish labels at the reference size so the floor is a known constant (−7.0 % at 0.1 SOL in `shared/walletScore.ts`'s cost model, −7.6 % under STATE-F's) and rankings do not move with the size picker ([shared/walletScore.ts](../shared/walletScore.ts)).

The features with any evidence behind them fall into three classes. Ranking keys are noise-penalised trailing follower returns: the WWW'26 paper's strongest wallet features were the average return over the 6th–10th prior coins, the return standard deviation and the t-statistic of the mean return, with purchase amount and time since first trade close behind ([arXiv:2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08641)); the poker result that a t-statistic ranking cut top-to-bottom decile flips from 12.3 % to 5.4 % is the same idea ([Potter van Loon et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0115479)); and internally the shrunk follower median with k = 10 was the least-bad of a dozen selections, scoring **−4.7 [−5.3, −4.2] against −6.3 [−8.7, −3.9] for the raw median and −9.9 for the baseline**, while the composite Copy score "adds nothing over follower return + follower win" ([docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)). Gates are traits that persist but do not rank: reachable share (ρ 0.63/0.79, "persistent, useless as a ranker") belongs below 25 % as an exclusion; the sub-10-second hold class is where "too fast bites" (reach collapsed from 82 % to 11 % between a 1 s and 8 s lag) while 10–60 s holds barely moved ([docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)). Labels are the stable style traits and their measured harm, which define the style buckets the prior is hierarchical over (three hold terciles × three size terciles × curve/PumpSwap entry). Entry timing relative to launch is a weak monotone coin-level feature at best: Bitquery's cross-section has 34 % of first-minute buys sold at a profit versus 28 % for buys a day later, and the same source finds more coins traded means more losers (71 % of one-coin buyers lost, 78 % of those with 21+ coins) ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-memecoin-traders-lose-money)). Three decay features deserve a column from day one because the Scout publicising a wallet is itself a crowding mechanism: the slope of the shrunk median over the last four windows, the change in the count of wallets buying within 2 s after the leader (the internal H3 shadow count, top-ranked negative-filter hypothesis in [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md §5](./best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md)), and the change in entry premium between the leader's fill and the first fillable print. Selectivity, sizing consistency, time-of-day regularity and sell discipline (disposition) appear in no published out-of-sample memecoin test; they are hypotheses to log, not signals to rank on.

### Adversarial filter layer

Of seventeen detectors catalogued, only four have any published accuracy, so the honest posture is to hard-exclude on proof-grade signals, warn on the rest, and let the scout's own held-out follower returns decide which warnings become exclusions. The proof-grade set is small and cheap. Bitquery's bundle rule, "bought inside the dev's launch transaction, the dev paid its fee, or its purchase came in the four transactions after the launch", was validated against the public Jito record: **185 of 186 buys in those four positions were in the launch bundle and none of 139 further down were**; funding-graph links added only 0.4 % more bundled launches because bundle wallets are dev-funded and already sit next to the create ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)). One `getBlock` on the creation slot settles it per (wallet, mint). Same-transaction round trips against one curve (WT1) are near-zero false positive because "MEV and cross-token arbitrage are not possible inside a bonding curve"; they sit on 8.26 % of coins in a 1 % sample and on 21.4 % of pre-migration transactions in MemeTrans's 30.8 million, and a wallet whose winners carry a high WT1 share of volume bought its own record ([arXiv:2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1); [arXiv:2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)). Looser wash rules explode: the same-coin-same-price test flags 12.4 % of coins at a 1 s window and 88.5 % at a day, "which the authors read as evidence of false positives" ([arXiv:2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)).

Snipers should become a hard exclusion rather than the current warning chip. Bitquery's 25,882 sniper wallets spent $440M for $31.5M after fees with 54 % ahead, against at least $579M for devs and their bundles; the dev's first sale came within a minute on 65 % of launches ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)); and 55 % of deployer-funded snipes were fully exited within one minute, 85 % within five ([Pine Analytics via Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604803448)). A 2 s follower of such a wallet buys after the informed seller has started selling, which is what the internal creator label measures as −16.2 % against −9.5 % ([docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)). The two features every source converges on are the share of a wallet's trips opened within five slots of the mint's first trade and the number of distinct creators among those mints.

Copy-farming now has named, traced cases and a transferable statistic. Bubblemaps' Anglio trace found five side wallets, funded from his public wallet, bought in **61 % of 534 calls within 20 seconds before the post and sold about four minutes after**, with proceeds flowing to matching exchange deposit addresses ([TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/34592)); a 770-call study found a 30-second entry delay cut the share of calls reaching +20 % from 56.4 % to 31.4 % and that median returns at fixed exits were −6.2 % at one minute and −10.5 % at five, with the mean at 30 s a tail-driven +552 % against a −4.0 % median ([moneyleavesclues](https://moneyleavesclues.substack.com/p/inside-the-economics-of-pumpfun-call)). The generalisation to copy bots is a follower-dependence test: for each leader win, the share of volume between the leader's buy and sell that came from wallets whose first buy on the mint was within N seconds of the leader's; a leader whose profit concentrates in high-shadow-share mints is farming whether or not it means to, and GMGN's own new callout score asks the same question, "does he sell into the attention his own call created" ([GMGNAI/gmgn-skills](https://github.com/GMGNAI/gmgn-skills)). Cluster membership should be computed, in order of cost, from shared fee payer or signer (proof-grade), the first inbound SOL transfer's sender and its transaction signature (grouping by funding transaction defeats single-use relay wallets where grouping by address does not: in the Rugburn case, six groups of one became one group of five ([Rugburn](https://rugburn.io/blog/funded-before-the-token-existed))), shared first funders across the leader and its winning mints' early buyers, and shared outbound exchange deposit targets. The only cluster method with a published robustness number is the CCS'26 first-funder graph, whose largest component kept a median 80.4 % of addresses under adversarial leave-one-out; that is robustness, not precision, and no Solana clustering paper reports precision against ownership, so the product wording is "linked" with the evidence shown, never "same owner" ([arXiv:2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246v1)). Exclude exchange, bridge and launchpad funders from links; Helius's `funded-by` returns the immediate funder with entity labels but is paid-plan only at 100 credits a call ([Helius funded-by](https://www.helius.dev/docs/wallet-api/funded-by)). A fresh wallet is unrated, not clean. Treat tokens received by transfer as unknown cost basis and their later sale as not a trip, which is what GMGN's own portfolio docs advise and what Bitquery did.

Rug exposure is a coin-level problem the scout should aggregate per leader rather than model itself: coin-level early-rug prediction reaches AUCPRC 0.80 in-venue but collapses to 0.58 when a pump.fun-trained model is applied to Raydium, so venue must be a feature or the split ([arXiv:2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20271v1)); MemeTrans's high-risk classifier reaches precision 0.846 and recall 0.833 on graduates ([arXiv:2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)); and wallets that paid for the first purchase of 100 or more coins account for 49 % of launches ([Bitquery](https://bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)). The per-leader statistics are the share of its buys on mints whose creator cluster had many prior launches and no graduation, and the share where bundled supply at launch exceeded 20 %; both are descriptive chips until held-out trips show they predict follower loss. Finally, sandwich exposure is a cost the follower controls through slippage, not wallet choice: Jupiter swaps into pump coins were sandwiched **8 per 10,000 at 1–3 % slippage, 87 at 10–20 % and 148 at 20–50 %**, 96 % of 6.85 million sandwiches hit pump.fun venues, only 6 % had buy and sell in the same slot so an own-fill detector must look ±8 slots, and `jitodontfront` was set on 98.5 % of Axiom's 21.5 million sandwiched victim transactions, so it must be added but never marketed as protection ([Bitquery sandwiches](https://bitquery.io/investigations/solana-sandwich-attacks); [arXiv:2609.28115](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28115v1)). The app's 15 % default sits in the 87-per-10k band, and large-size leaders impose a systematic sandwich tax on followers over and above the entry premium, which makes the "Large size" chip a sandwich-exposure chip as well.

### Ranking and shrinkage

The repo's shrunk median, (n·med + 10·prior)/(n + 10), is a normal-normal empirical-Bayes posterior mean in disguise with k = σ²_trade/τ²_wallet, the same device fund selection converged on when it treated individual alphas as draws from a population and found past performance "a weak guide" mainly because of estimation noise ([Harvey & Liu, RFS 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v31y2018i7p2499-2552..html); [Jones & Shanken, JFE 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v78y2005i3p507-552.html)). The principled upgrade is to make both constants measured quantities each week: σ̂² as the pooled within-wallet variance of robust-scaled per-copy returns, τ̂² as the between-wallet variance of means minus the sampling contribution, floored at a small positive value, and μ as the universe's population median rather than a fixed −15.5 %, because the prior moved to −18.2, −19.5, −19.0 and −18.0 across the other four tapes and the earlier −9.9 % "pulled thin, lucky records UP the ranking" ([shared/walletScore.ts](../shared/walletScore.ts); [docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)). Plausible dispersions (σ 30–40 points, τ 8–12) give k between 6 and 25, bracketing the current 10; σ 40 with τ 5 gives 64, which would say shrink nearly everything. Because the tails are heavy, shrink the median with its own standard error, 0.455·IQR/√n under a logistic shape, or fit a Student-t location with ν ≈ 3–5.

Rank on a lower bound, not the posterior mean. The winner's-curse literature shows selected winners carry upward-biased estimates and invalid intervals and that shrinking toward the grand mean before picking the maximum is the fix ([arXiv:2603.12867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12867)); pessimistic (lower-confidence-bound) selection is adaptively minimax-optimal when there is no exploration budget and the user bears every loss ([Rashidinejad et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/60ce36723c17bbac504f2ef4c8a46995-Abstract.html)); and internally the decile-10 overfit is exactly what an LCB with z between 0.84 and 1.28 penalises, because decile 10 is filled with small-n high-mean wallets. The hierarchical layer is a two-level prior by style bucket, shrinking a wallet toward its bucket's mean and the bucket toward the population, justified by style being a near-fixed trait and bucket outcomes differing by 3–7 points ([docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)). For a top-N with a false-discovery guarantee, conformal selection wraps any predictor, builds a conformal p-value per candidate against the null "next-week median ≤ fee floor" and applies Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.2; the selection set may be empty, which is the formal version of "recommend nobody" ([Jin & Candès, JMLR 2023](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10538790)). Weekly regime drift breaks exchangeability, so calibrate on the last 4–8 weeks or use the weighted variant. Choose k, z and q by walk-forward combinatorial purged cross-validation and report the probability of backtest overfitting over the whole variant grid, treating any variant with PBO above 0.5 as noise, because the dozen variants already tried are themselves a multiple-testing exposure ([Bailey et al., PBO](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [Purged cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)). Minimum-sample rules follow from the dispersion: a wallet's own median at n = 10 has a standard error near 8.6–10 points with an IQR of 60–70, so rank only at ≥ 10 trips, shrink hard below about 80 copies, and move the "reliable" badge from 20 copies to about 80 until the IQR is measured. Walk-forward weeks are UTC Monday boundaries; the universe is frozen at the boundary as every wallet with ≥ 10 copied trips in the feature window including wallets that go silent (conditioning on activity in the label week is the look-ahead that makes a leaderboard look alive); feature-window trips whose leader sell lands in the test week are purged and trips opened in the last ten minutes before the boundary are embargoed so a settle-at-last-mark leg cannot peek forward.

Model classes come in stages and each must beat the previous in paired walk-forward. Stage 0 is the EB-shrunk median with weekly (μ, k) and an LCB. Stage 1 is a monotone-constrained LightGBM with at most ten features whose signs are fixed in advance (reachable % positive; creator, large size, shadow count, recent drawdown and dead-coin share negative; slope of shrunk median and hold-time stability positive), depth ≤ 3, strong L2 and ≥ 50 observations per leaf, because monotonicity is the regularisation that stops a 300-row-per-week model from learning tape idiosyncrasies ([LightGBM parameters](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Parameters.html)). Stage 2, learning-to-rank with one query group per week, waits for at least 30 weekly groups, since its evidence comes from equity cross-sections with thousands of groups and whether LightGBM honours monotone constraints under `lambdarank` is not stated in the docs ([Poh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07149)). The memecoin-specific warning is that XGBoost had the highest AUC in the WWW'26 paper and still produced negative copier returns, and White Whale's neural net raised the doubling rate from 10 % to 26 % and lost every day ([arXiv:2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08641); [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2297445)); a better classifier of coins is not a positive copier.

### Leader rotation and sizing

The frame should be a continuously re-qualified basket with pessimistic promotion, not a weekly hunt for one wallet, because every source that picks the single best past performer finds the curse. Across fourteen optimising portfolio models and seven datasets none consistently beat equal weights, and mean-variance would need about 3,000 months of data for 25 assets to catch up ([DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html)); among 6,000 hedge funds "a small number of funds is sufficient to reap most of the diversification benefits" ([Lhabitant & Learned](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ris/jofitr/1342.html)). With equal weights, 5 leaders remove 80 % of diversifiable variance, 8 remove 87.5 % and 10 remove 90 %, so 5–8 is the target and anything beyond adds credits and convergence exposure. Diversification lowers variance, not the median: a basket of negative-median wallets loses more predictably, not less. Because followed leaders converge on the same coins, the effective number of bets is the number of distinct mints, and the convergence curve makes the rule unambiguous: **one open copy per mint across all follows, and no entry when another followed wallet already holds or bought the mint in the last five minutes**.

The allocation problem has three layers with three different methods, because paper follows reveal every shortlisted wallet's return at near-zero cost. The weekly shortlist-to-paper cut is fixed-budget best-arm identification: start 20–30 shortlisted wallets all in paper and run Sequential Halving, dropping the bottom half by the lower bound of a clipped mean (returns clipped to [−1, +3] for the estimator only, so one +1,461 % print cannot promote a wallet) on days 2, 4 and 6 ([Sequential Halving survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.02606)). Paper-to-live is a conservative bandit whose baseline arm is copying nobody at 0 %: promote only when the 95 % lower bound of the clipped mean clears a hurdle of about +3 % net (fees plus slippage allowance) on at least 20 paper copies in the window, and withdraw live capital the moment the bound falls back below the baseline ([Wu, Shariff, Lattimore & Szepesvári, ICML 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.04282)). Live weights are proportional to max(0, LCB) divided by robust scale, capped at 30 % of the live book per leader with the live book at most 30 % of bankroll, which is equal-risk contribution with a pessimistic tilt. The non-stationary bandit theory brackets the lookback between a couple of days and the whole week and admits it cannot choose inside that range when the number of changes is unknown ([Garivier & Moulines](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.3415)); the internal reliability curve sets the floor at ~20 copies, so a 48–72 h window with a ~2-day half-life, re-tuned on the tape, is the default, and the rotation clock should run in copies observed rather than calendar days so a 50-copies-a-day wallet and a 5-copies-a-week wallet are not judged on the same window.

Sizing follows from the sign of the edge. For small fractions the Kelly fraction is roughly μ/σ², and with μ negative in every measured cohort **the Kelly answer today is zero**; any live size while the paper lower bound is below the hurdle is a deliberate loss paid for information and should be labelled a probe (0.01–0.03 SOL). When a wallet's bound does clear, for example μ̂ = +3 % and robust σ̂ = 60 % per copy, full Kelly is about 8.3 % of bankroll per copy and quarter-Kelly 2.1 %, or 0.21 SOL on a 10 SOL copy bankroll. Quarter rather than half because the estimate comes from at most a week of paper on a distribution with kurtosis far above the excess kurtosis of ≈ 2 measured for social-trading providers ([Doering, Neumann & Paul](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0306_fullpaper.pdf)), and because the Kelly table makes the asymmetry explicit: half-Kelly gives a 0.89 probability of doubling before halving at 75 % of full growth, while 700 wagers at a 14 % advantage can still turn $1,000 into $18 under full Kelly against $145 under half ([MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)). Keep the proportional "percent of the leader's trade" rule for mirroring sells, cap the copy at the Kelly-derived amount and at a small share of the coin's SOL reserves so the follower's own buy is not the price move it then sells into, and use MAD or IQR of clipped returns as the scale rather than a Gaussian σ.

Retirement should trigger on observable structural change, with return drawdown kept only as a capital breaker, because firing on past returns replaces noise with noise ([Goyal & Wahal](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i4p1805-1847.html); [Cornell, Hsu & Nanigian](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20180321-095702727)) while leaders demonstrably change behaviour once followed: becoming a first-time leader on eToro increases the disposition effect in a difference-in-differences design ([Pelster & Hofmann, JBF 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v94y2018icp75-88.html)). In order of evidence strength: style drift (a trait with ρ ≥ 0.83 does not double by chance, so if median hold or median size over the last 20 trips moves by more than 2×, |log ratio| > 0.7, or the under-60 s share moves 25 points, pause to paper); crowding (if the 24-hour median count of distinct buyers landing within 2 s after the leader's buys doubles against its 7-day baseline, the lag cost will rise); inactivity (85 % of profitable wallets trade again the next day, so demote at 48 h and drop at 7 days ([docs/wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md](./wallet-convergence-2026-09-14.md))); the 40 trades-per-minute bot rule; a drawdown breaker set from the wallet's own paper distribution (pause at 2× its worst 7-day paper day) that never re-ranks; and losing streaks only at eight or more, because at a 35 % win rate five straight losses is a weekly event for every honest wallet (0.65⁵ = 11.6 %) while eight is 3.2 %. Every live wallet re-clears the promotion rule weekly or is soft-retired: no new buys, mirrored sells continue on open positions, max-hold backstop, seven-day cooldown. Soft retirement matters because mirroring sells beats fixed holds internally, so an orphaned position loses its exit signal, and a turnover cap of about two promotions and two retirements a week respects both the √S regret cost of following a changing leader ([Herbster & Warmuth, via arXiv:1909.02187](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.02187)) and the 1.2 pp/yr behaviour gap investors pay for timing ([Morningstar Mind the Gap 2025](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap-2025)). Do not use a ranked "best wallet" list as the promotion mechanism in the UI; promote on the rule and display the rule's outcome.

### Self-scoring: the scorecard that makes the scout's skill measurable

The board already records picks against everyone (`SCOUT_PICKS_N = 10`, `SCOUT_PICK_KEEP_DAYS = 14`, `SCOUT_RECORD_MIN_COPIES = 20`, pooled median and win rate of trips opened after the pick), but it pools copies across picks and across the whole follow span, so the paired tests below cannot be computed without a per-week schema ([shared/walletScout.ts](../shared/walletScout.ts)). Each calendar week should produce one row with the top-N realised (pooled median, 5 %-winsorised mean, win rate, total SOL at reference size, copies, picks that traded) and six controls on the same week and universe: the universe baseline; random-N wallets redrawn 200 times with the 5th–95th percentile band (the top-N must sit above the band, which is the order-statistic null); random buys matched on curve position, hour and the picks' hold distribution; the leader-PnL top-N as the "what everyone else copies" proxy; a decile-9 selection; and last week's top-N held another week, which prices rotation itself. Paired differences carry wallet-clustered bootstrap intervals; a running paired test over weeks decides when a gap is resolved, and with the population median moving 1.6 points across five tapes and decile medians 1–3 points between periods, a 4-point gap between two selections needs roughly 3–8 paired weeks and a 2-point gap about four times that ([docs/wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md §4](./wallet-finder-research-2026-10-22.md)).

Three diagnostics separate skill from luck faster than outcomes do. Calibration: regress realised median on the pick-time shrunk median across all picks; a slope below one means the ranker is under-shrunk and k should rise, a slope near one with a negative intercept means a stale prior and μ should fall. Rank quality: the week's Spearman between pick-time score and realised median over the whole universe, not just the top-N, with the half-day 0.48 as the reference. Process: for every follower fill, the mark 30 s and 5 min after the fill before any exit, the memecoin analogue of closing-line value, because a selection whose picks are immediately under water is paying for crowding regardless of outcome and the statistic converges in hundreds of fills rather than tens of weeks. Track decay by days since pick (0–2, 3–7, 8–14) and by change in shadow count; a monotone fall is the board's own alpha decay, which the Scout should expect because publicising a wallet is itself a crowding mechanism. Three honesty switches belong on the dashboard: "recommend nobody" when the best probability of clearing the floor is below 0.5 or the conformal set is empty, "not enough weeks" until the paired test has the weeks the observed dispersion requires, and the current ranker's PBO over its variant grid. Pool across weeks only as medians of weekly medians, never as a grand pooled copy median, or one hyperactive pick with 2,160 trips dominates. The pipeline's own KPI is promotion precision: the share of promoted wallets with a positive next-week realised paper return, a number no copy-trading product or study has ever published.

## The pre-registered test that settles whether it works

Everything above can be built without knowing the answer; this is the experiment that produces it. It reuses tools that already exist (the chain-slot pricer, STATE-F, slot telemetry with `CopyTiming.leaderSlot/ourSlot/slotGap`, the hold-matched random control) and precedents the team has already honoured, since the lag study fixed P1–P5 before computing and the exits study's PREREG.md predates its first output by file time ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md §0](./swarm-2026-10-23/lag.md); [docs/swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md §3](./swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md); [docs/best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md](./best-copy-trader-research-2026-10-21.md)).

Freeze on Sunday 00:00 UTC of week W by writing and hashing `PREREG-W.md` before any W+1 data exists. The selection rule is the Stage 0 ranker: shrunk follower median at the Scout's measured lag under STATE-F, at least 10 copyable trips in the trailing seven days, bot rule under 40 trades a minute, hard exclusions from the adversarial layer, top-10 (TOP). Freeze alongside it RAND, ten wallets drawn at random from the eligible set stratified on trip count and median first-buy size; LEAD, the top-10 of Kolscan or GMGN by 7-day leader PnL, snapshotted manually if programmatic reads are barred ([kolscan.io/leaderboard](https://kolscan.io/leaderboard)); FLOOR at −7.60 % for 0.1 SOL; and NONE at 0 %. In week W+1 paper-follow all three wallet arms identically and never re-rank: 0.1 SOL per copy, lag equal to the session's measured slot gap (report its median; if no live telemetry exists, state the nine-slot assumption and run a few 0.01 SOL live copies in week one to measure it), mirrored sells with `minLeaderSellPct` 50, at most three open positions per wallet, every leg chain-priced with the slot rule, dead and never-closed trips at the last mark, too-fast trips recorded as skipped and counted. Record per copy the wallet, mint, venue, leader slot, our slot, both fills, exit schedule, net return and whether the Scout had seen the mint.

The primary endpoint is the pooled median net return per copy of TOP over eight consecutive weeks with a mint-clustered bootstrap 95 % interval; success is a lower bound above zero, which is above the fee floor by construction. Secondaries are the mean and 1 %-trimmed mean weekly P&L in SOL with a 95 % lower bound above zero, the TOP − RAND and TOP − LEAD differences with intervals excluding zero in the positive direction, and a win rate against cost above 50 %. About 300 copies a week gives roughly 2,400 copies and a minimum detectable median effect near 2.5 points. Stop for futility at four weeks if the pooled TOP upper bound is below zero; never stop early for success, because optional stopping inflates false positives; and if more than one rule, size or venue is tried, demand the equivalent of t ≈ 3 for the headline or Bonferroni across arms ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)). What convinces a sceptic is eight pre-registered weeks, a hashed rule, chain pricing, a same-week random control, a published count of rules tried, and a TOP median lower bound above zero on the pooled sample **and** positive in at least six of eight individual weeks.

Two additions turn a pass/fail into a diagnosis. The ablation re-prices every TOP trip at 0, 1, 2, 4 and 9 slots. If 0-slot copying is positive and 9-slot is negative, the barrier is latency and the honest product claim is "not achievable from a desktop"; if even 0-slot is at or below zero and RAND is statistically indistinguishable from TOP, the barrier is selection and co-location would not help either. The rank-decay track follows each week's TOP through W+1, W+2 and W+3 and computes the Spearman of their shrunk medians against the W ranking: ρ ≥ 0.4 that holds at W+3 is a trait, ρ falling to zero by W+2 is noise, and this is the direct test of whether weekly re-selection is a feature or a symptom; record alongside it how many of TOP read above zero on the Scout versus the full-week chain replay, which quantifies the survivorship gap. The second arm, pre-registered separately, restricts the eligible set to wallets whose trips are at least 80 % post-migration and tests 0.1 and 0.5 SOL, because PumpSwap/Raydium is the one condition with a mechanical reason to differ and no evidence either way. Cost is about 600 credits per 30-trip replay, roughly 60k credits for three arms of ten wallets and about 100 trips each, inside the free 1M Helius budget, with no funds moved and nothing signed ([docs/copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md](./copy-candidates-2026-10-24.md)).

The decision tree is written in advance. If the curve arm's lower bound clears zero in ≥ 6 of 8 weeks, build the P2 basket pipeline and the whole-market backend, and still ship the floor beside every number. If the curve arm fails but the PumpSwap arm clears, the product becomes a post-migration copier with the curve as a vetting-only surface. If both fail at the measured slot gap but the 0-slot ablation is positive, the honest claim is latency-bound and the roadmap question is co-location, not ranking. If both fail even at 0 slots, the measured evidence says the edge is not there to be found by selection, and the defensible positions are the ones already supported: a follower-scored board that can recommend nobody and shows the floor (measured to halve the loss against leader-PnL ranking, still negative), a wallet-vetting tool built on labels with measured stability and harm, and a paper-first trainer whose value is the counterfactual record, with every "profitable copy" claim, speed spend and convergence default removed ([docs/swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md Q9](./swarm-2026-10-23/skeptic.md)).

## Conclusion

The ambition and the evidence meet at one point: the scout's job is not to find the wallet but to prove, week by week and against controls, whether any wallet is worth following at all. Everything the measurements allow a ranker to achieve on the curve is already visible in the five-to-seven-point gap between all ranked wallets and the top decile, and that gap ends at the fee floor; the next increment cannot come from a smarter ordering of curve wallets, only from a venue where fee and impact are lower, from a size that dilutes the fixed cost, or from a latency a desktop does not have. That reframes "best copy-trading software in the world" from a promise about returns into a property of the instrument: the only tool whose numbers are the follower's own, chain-complete, shrunk against a weekly-measured prior, ranked on a lower bound, scored against random and leaderboard baselines on the same week, and able to say nobody. No competitor has that property, the academic literature on copy trading says it is precisely what followers lack, and it is fully measured today.

The weekly rotation the team has been living is, read correctly, data rather than a workflow: it is the rate at which a 30-trip median regresses to a −15.5 % prior, and the rank-decay track turns that feeling into a Spearman coefficient within three weeks. The same reframing applies to leader rotation and sizing, where the Kelly answer is zero until a lower bound clears a hurdle, and to adversarial filtering, where the proof-grade signals are cheap and the rest are hypotheses the held-out record will grade. Eight pre-registered weeks and about 60k free credits settle the question that two swarms, three replays and a full-week chain check have so far only bounded; whatever the answer, the product that runs that test honestly is a better product than any that does not.
